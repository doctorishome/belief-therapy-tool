Belief Counseling for Counselors

The Process

Three steps: Identify, Challenge, and Replace. The result: the truth sets you free.

The flow of Belief Counseling

Every client moves through the same path. Belief Mining uncovers the beliefs; the three steps change them.

Each part of the flow

Belief Mining

Start with the presenting problem, then gather the whole story and look for the beliefs beneath it at six levels:

  • Biological
  • Psychological/Cognitive
  • Interpersonal
  • Socioeconomic
  • Sociocultural
  • Spiritual/Religious

Why: You cannot find the right belief until you know the person.

1. Identify False Beliefs

  • Put the specific false belief into words, in the client’s own language.
  • Check it against the 22 Core False Beliefs and the Spiritual/Religious False Beliefs.
  • Choose the belief that, if changed, would bring the greatest relief.

Why: You cannot challenge a belief you haven’t named.

2. Challenge False Beliefs

  • Questioning: ask general and Christian questions that let the client examine the belief for themselves.
  • Refuting: weigh the belief against evidence, Scripture, logic, and the client’s own experience.
  • Behavioral experiments: do something differently and see what actually happens.

Why: A belief held for years needs more than a gentle doubt.

3. Replace False Beliefs

  • Replace the lie with biblical truth, such as “I am imperfect, but deeply loved by God.”
  • Use the Scriptural Truths for the belief, and Pearls Scripture Search for more.
  • Practice the truth with homework: Scripture memory, reminder cards, songs, listening, and journaling.
  • Review progress, belief strength, and remaining triggers.

Why: Removing a lie leaves a gap; truth must take its place, and beliefs change through repetition.

The Truth Sets You Free

Freedom is not a fourth step. It is what happens when a lie is identified, challenged, and replaced with God’s truth.
