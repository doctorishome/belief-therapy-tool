Belief Counseling for Counselors

The Process

The eight steps of Belief Therapy, and the reason behind each one.

The eight steps

Each step has a purpose. In training, you will learn what to do and why it works.

1
Assess

Gather the whole story: the presenting problem, family and parental patterns, Christian background, and functioning across the biological, psychological, interpersonal, socioeconomic, sociocultural, and spiritual levels.

Why: You cannot find the right belief until you know the person.

2
Identify

Surface the specific false beliefs driving the distress, in the client’s own words.

Why: You cannot attack a belief you haven’t named.

3
Hypothesize

Decide which belief, if changed, would bring the greatest shift.

Why: Not every lie carries equal weight; start where change matters most.

4
Question

Ask spiritual and general questions that invite the client to examine the belief for themselves.

Why: Questions let clients discover the flaw instead of being told.

5
Refute

Argue against the false belief with evidence, Scripture, logic, and the client’s own experience.

Why: A belief held for years needs more than a gentle doubt.

6
Replace

Build a replacement belief grounded in biblical truth, such as “I am imperfect but deeply loved by God.”

Why: Removing a lie leaves a gap; truth must take its place.

7
Rehearse

Assign homework: Scripture memory, reminder cards, reading, listening, and small behavioral experiments.

Why: Beliefs change through repetition.

8
Reinforce

Review progress, belief strength, remaining triggers, and spiritual growth.

Why: Gains last when they are reviewed and protected.
