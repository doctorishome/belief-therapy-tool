Step 2: Challenge

Cognitive Restructuring

Catch, check, and change the automatic thoughts that grow out of a false belief.

Cognitive restructuring is the core skill of cognitive therapy. It helps clients notice the automatic thoughts that run through their minds, examine them honestly, and replace distorted thoughts with ones that are accurate and true. In Belief Counseling, it is the bridge between Challenge and Replace. Questioning loosens the hold of a false belief, and restructuring turns what the client discovers into new, truthful thinking they can use every day.

Three steps: Catch, Check, Change

1. Catch the thought

Help the client notice what they tell themselves in hard moments. Ask, “What was going through your mind just then?” Have them write the situation, the thought, the feeling, and how strongly they believed the thought (0–100%).

2. Check the thought

Examine the thought together instead of arguing with it.

  • What is the evidence for it, and against it?
  • Which cognitive errors are at work, such as all-or-nothing thinking, labeling, or discounting the positive?
  • Where did I learn this?
  • What would I tell a friend who thought this?
  • What does God say about this?

3. Change the thought

Help the client write a balanced, truthful alternative in their own words. Then have them rate how strongly they believe the new thought, and how strongly they now believe the old one.

Example. “I’m a failure” might become: “I made a costly mistake, and I’m responsible for my part. A mistake does not make me a failure, and God has not given up on me.”

A few cautions

  • It is not positive thinking. The goal is truth, not cheerfulness. Some painful thoughts are accurate, and those call for problem-solving and support, not correction.
  • Work together. Questions help the client discover the truth for themselves. Clients hold on to what they discover far better than what they are told.
  • Expect practice. New thoughts feel weak at first. Thought records between sessions, repeated over weeks, are what make the change last.
  • Timing matters. Restructure only after you understand the belief and the client feels heard (Guideline 1).

The biblical picture

Scripture calls believers to examine their thinking and bring it under the truth:

“We tear down arguments and every presumption set up against the knowledge of God; and we take captive every thought to make it obedient to Christ.” (2 Corinthians 10:5, BSB)

Cognitive restructuring is a practical way to take thoughts captive. Each false thought is caught, tested against the evidence and against God’s Word, and replaced with what is true.
