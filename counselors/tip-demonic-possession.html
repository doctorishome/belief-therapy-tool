My Thoughts on Demonic Possession

What 35 years with the most disturbed clients taught me

Dr. Brian Campbell (2026)

← All Professional Tips
Note: This tip shares my personal experience and opinions as a Christian psychologist. It is not a theological ruling or a substitute for a medical evaluation.

A former student once asked me whether demons play a role in bipolar disorder. I thought all of you would benefit from hearing my answer.

One Case in 35 Years

In more than 35 years of working with people with mental health problems of every kind, I have seen only one case that I felt may have been demonic possession.

Keep in mind that I have worked with some of the most disturbed people on the planet. I even worked with patients Castro sent out of Cuba from the back wards of his country’s terrible mental institutions. I have treated people who banged their heads against the wall hundreds of times a day, who inserted glass into their own bodies, who bit off other patients’ fingers and noses, who drank their own urine, and who swallowed bolts, paper clips, and nails (a condition called pica). I think you get the point.

You may be surprised to hear that in the overwhelming majority of these cases, treatment was extremely successful. Almost all of these individuals were eventually able to move into group homes in the community. We “failed” in only one or two cases.

The One Case

One of those cases was a young woman who injured herself, and I specialized in treating self-injury. Her case was so severe that she was kept in physical restraints in the back ward of a state hospital. When she was untied, she would strike her head hundreds of times a day on the floor, the corners of tables, and anything else nearby.

We brought her into our behavior-training unit and used every technique that had worked with others, but she never really improved. At one point she began speaking in voices that sounded very much demonic. A therapist friend said to me:

“You know, she sounds like she is possessed.”

She did not respond to medication, and she did not respond to the best behavioral treatment available anywhere. I have to conclude that she may have been possessed.

Possession Is Extremely Rare

So in my experience, demonic possession is extremely rare, even in the back wards of a state hospital. In my 25 years as a Christian counselor in private practice, with hundreds, perhaps thousands, of clients, I never saw another case. That includes clients with bipolar disorder, autism, psychotic disorders, and more. Every client with bipolar disorder I have worked with responded to medication, cognitive-behavioral therapy, or both.

When a Church Saw Demons Everywhere

At one point in my career, I was asked to give a presentation at a large local church. Its counseling ministry consisted almost entirely of “casting out demons.” This troubled me deeply, because the counselors were using that approach with people who obviously had schizophrenia. When the deliverance sessions didn’t work, the church would eventually send these people to me.

In almost every case, with the right medication and Christian cognitive-behavioral therapy (CCBT), they improved significantly.

Interestingly, the church’s pastor said that in more than 20 years of ministry, he had seen only one case of what he believed was true possession. I was delighted when he told the people I was teaching:

“There is no demon of gluttony; it’s just that people eat too much.”

He went on to say:

“I have only seen one case of true possession in my career. If you are seeing demon possession in every client you see, you need to come and see me for help.”

A Practical Test

Before considering demonic possession

  1. Make sure the person has had a thorough medical and psychiatric evaluation. Seizures, brain injury, infections, and drug effects can all produce frightening symptoms.
  2. Provide appropriate treatment: medication where indicated, plus expert Christian counseling (CCBT), behavior modification, or traditional cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT).
  3. Watch the response. If the person improves significantly, there is no reason to believe he or she is possessed.
  4. Only if the person does not respond to any of these may there be reason to consider possession, and then in consultation with mature pastoral leadership.

In my experience, response to treatment is one of the most helpful clues we have. Conversely, if the client responds significantly to appropriate medication and expert therapy, there is no reason to think demons are involved.

Can a Christian Be Possessed?

Personally, I do not believe the evil one can possess those who are Spirit-filled Christians. He can try to influence us, but I do not believe he can possess us.

1 John 4:4 “You, little children, are from God and have overcome them, because greater is He who is in you than he who is in the world.”
