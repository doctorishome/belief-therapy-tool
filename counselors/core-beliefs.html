Belief Counseling for Counselors

Core False Beliefs

The deep-seated lies beneath much of our pain: lies about ourselves, other people, and the world.

About core false beliefs

Belief Mining can help uncover faulty beliefs that influence a client’s thoughts, emotions, and behavior. Among the beliefs discovered, some may warrant particular attention because they shape many areas of the client’s life.

These core beliefs are deeply held convictions about oneself, other people, and the world. Beliefs such as “I am worthless” or “I am not lovable” can feel like unquestionable truths to clients, influencing how they interpret experiences and respond to everyday situations.

Although exploring every underlying belief may not be practical, identifying a client’s most influential core beliefs provides a useful focus for further work.

The table below presents dysfunctional core beliefs that research has linked with psychological difficulties, including depression, anxiety, and relationship problems. These beliefs may contribute to the development or persistence of emotional distress.

The associations shown can help you and your client explore a belief’s possible influence; they do not, by themselves, indicate a diagnosis of a psychological disorder.

Identifying these beliefs leads into the Challenge phase of Belief Counseling, where you help the client examine their accuracy and test their influence.

Clicking a core belief in the table opens a page that describes that belief. From there, you can continue to Questioning Beliefs, where you will find questions tailored to help the client evaluate the belief and consider alternative perspectives.

A separate Behavioral Challenges page will offer practical activities that let clients test their beliefs through experience.

Together, questioning and behavioral challenges can help clients loosen the hold of dysfunctional beliefs and prepare to replace them with healthier, more truthful beliefs contained in the Bible.

Digging deeper

Many false beliefs are deep-seated and are difficult to unearth. For discovering deeper toxic beliefs, also see: Parental Messages.

Core false beliefs do not belong to any one level of Belief Mining. They often sit underneath the beliefs a client first describes, so they are worth looking for in every case.

The 22 core false beliefs

Grouped by theme. Each belief opens its own belief card, where the belief is challenged and replaced with truth from Scripture.

My Worth

  • Something is wrong with me.
  • I am worthless.
  • I am not lovable.
  • I am a failure.

My Strength

  • I am powerless.
  • I cannot handle this.
  • I cannot make it on my own.

Other People

  • I don’t belong.
  • People will leave me.
  • No one will care for me when I need them.
  • Nobody loves me.
  • People cannot be trusted.
  • My needs don’t matter.

The World & the Future

  • Things will always go badly.
  • The world is unsafe.
  • Something terrible is going to happen.

Rules I Live By

  • I must be perfect.
  • I must have everyone’s approval.
  • I must be in control.
  • I must not make mistakes.

After Painful Experiences

  • It was my fault.
  • What happened makes me dirty or bad.
