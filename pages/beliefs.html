Beliefs?

What Is a Belief?

A belief is something we consciously or unconsciously accept or assume to be true.

Our beliefs help us make sense of ourselves, other people, God, and the world around us. They influence:

  • What we notice
  • How we interpret our experiences
  • What we expect from the future
  • How we think and talk to ourselves
  • How we feel and behave

Believing Something Does Not Make It True

Some beliefs are accurate and helpful. Others are incomplete, distorted, or simply false.

  • “I’m a failure.”
  • “Nobody could really love me.”
  • “People cannot be trusted.”
  • “Something terrible is going to happen.”
  • “I must please everyone to be accepted.”

These beliefs may feel completely true—especially when they have been held for many years. But feelings, repetition, and sincerity do not establish truth.

A belief can influence your entire life without ever having been carefully examined.

Where Do Our Beliefs Come From?

Beliefs develop gradually through our experiences and relationships. They may be shaped by:

  • Parents and caregivers
  • Teachers, peers, and churches
  • Successes, failures, rejection, and trauma
  • Culture, media, and economic circumstances
  • Physical health and biological factors
  • Religious experiences and our understanding of God

Some beliefs are directly taught to us. Others are conclusions we draw from what happens to us.

A child who is repeatedly criticized may eventually conclude, “There is something wrong with me.” A belief that developed during childhood may continue influencing that person’s emotions, relationships, and decisions many years later.

For example, the title of a popular book for the treatment of Attention Deficit Hyperactive Disorder (ADHD) is: “You Mean I’m Not Lazy, Stupid or Crazy.” People with ADHD in childhood often have to deal with negative labels and false beliefs throughout their lives.

Beliefs Shape Our Lives

Underlying beliefs can be compared to an ocean tide. The tide may be deep and difficult to see, but it influences everything moving on the surface.

In much the same way, our beliefs influence:

  • Thoughts
  • Emotions
  • Behavior
  • Perceptions
  • Self-Talk
  • Relationships
  • Spiritual life

An important question: What did I come to believe because of what happened to me?

Beliefs Can Change

We do not have to remain imprisoned by beliefs that are inaccurate or harmful. We can learn to identify them, examine where they came from, evaluate whether they are true, and replace false beliefs with truth.

For Christians, this process includes examining our beliefs in the light of Scripture and allowing God’s truth to renew our minds.
