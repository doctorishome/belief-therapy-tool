Behavioral Experiments

Changing what you believe by changing what you do

Sometimes questioning a false belief is not enough. We may understand that a belief is inaccurate and still find ourselves thinking and acting as though it were true.

One of the best ways to challenge a false belief is to do something differently and see what happens. This is called a behavioral experiment.

What Is a Behavioral Experiment?

A behavioral experiment is a simple activity that helps you test whether something you believe is actually true.

For example, suppose you believe:

“Nobody likes me.”

Instead of simply accepting this belief, you might start a friendly conversation with someone at church, at work, or in another familiar setting.

Then ask yourself: What happened? Did the person reject you, or did they respond warmly? Was the experience different from what you expected?

Even if the conversation doesn’t go well, you can still learn something by considering other possible explanations. Perhaps the person was busy, distracted, or having a hard day.

How to Conduct Your Own Behavioral Experiment

  1. Identify the false belief.What are you telling yourself that may not be true?
  2. Predict what will happen.If you try something different, what do you think will happen?
  3. Try a small experiment.Choose a safe, practical activity that will help you test your belief.
  4. Look at the results.What actually happened? Was your prediction correct? What did you learn?
  5. Practice a healthier belief.Use what you learned to develop a more accurate and helpful way of thinking.

Replacing Unhealthy Habits With Healthy Ones

You can also challenge false beliefs by changing how you spend your time. For example, if you spend hours worrying about the future, you might replace some of that time with activities that encourage healthier thinking:

  • Read a Christian devotional or a passage of Scripture.
  • Listen to encouraging Christian music.
  • Spend time with supportive friends or family.
  • Attend church or take part in a church activity.
  • Help someone who needs encouragement.

These activities can help you build healthier patterns of thinking and behavior.

Putting Your Faith Into Action

The Bible reminds us:

“Do not merely listen to the word, and so deceive yourselves. Do what it says.”James 1:22 (NIV)

Changing our beliefs often involves changing what we do. As we practice new ways of thinking and acting, we create opportunities to strengthen our faith and develop healthier habits.

Try It Yourself!

Many of the False Belief Cards on this website include homework exercises and behavioral experiments.

Choose an experiment that fits your situation. Start small, pay attention to what happens, and practice what you learn.

Remember: You don’t have to change everything at once. Small steps can lead to meaningful change.

These activities are intended for personal growth and education. They are not a substitute for professional counseling. Avoid any experiment that could put you or others at risk.
