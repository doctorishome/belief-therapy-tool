1. The Forms to Complete card

At the end of the session, the counselor opens the Forms to Complete card (on the Forms page) and checks the forms this client should complete before the next session. The counselor then either creates one link for the client, which opens only the checked forms, or prints the card for a client who uses paper.

What the client sees: a page titled "Your Forms" with a Start button for each form. Each question has large, easy-to-tap answer buttons. When a form is finished, it shows "Done." The client does not see scores. If the client indicates thoughts of self-harm, crisis information appears immediately (call or text 988, call 911, or go to the nearest emergency room).

What the counselor sees: each score, its severity range, and the client's rating of how much the problems interfere with daily life. A red safety flag appears if PHQ-9 question 9 is answered above "Not at all."

Current status: practice sample only. Use fictional clients. Answers are not saved or sent. Real client use will require a secure, HIPAA-compliant system and a way to alert the counselor promptly when a safety flag appears.

2. Symptom screens

About 70% of clients present with anxiety or depression, so these two short screens are the most common follow-up forms. Both ask about the last 2 weeks, with answers scored 0 (Not at all), 1 (Several days), 2 (More than half the days), and 3 (Nearly every day). Both are public domain: developed by Drs. Robert L. Spitzer, Janet B.W. Williams, Kurt Kroenke and colleagues, with an educational grant from Pfizer Inc. No permission is required to reproduce, translate, display, or distribute them.

PHQ-9 (depression): 9 questions, about 2 minutes. Total 0 to 27.
- 0 to 4: minimal
- 5 to 9: mild
- 10 to 14: moderate
- 15 to 19: moderately severe
- 20 to 27: severe
Question 9 asks about thoughts of being better off dead or of hurting oneself. Any answer above "Not at all" calls for prompt follow-up to assess safety; do not wait for the next session.

GAD-7 (anxiety): 7 questions, about 2 minutes. Total 0 to 21.
- 0 to 4: minimal
- 5 to 9: mild
- 10 to 14: moderate
- 15 to 21: severe

Scores are screening results, not a diagnosis. Confirm with the clinical interview and DSM-5-TR criteria. Repeating the screens later in treatment shows change over time.

3. Optional spiritual screens (if the client has chosen to include faith)

RSS-5, Religious and Spiritual Struggles Scale-5 (Salcone et al., 2025). Offer routinely. Five items, about 2 minutes, one for each kind of struggle: doubt, struggle with God, ultimate meaning, interpersonal (with religious people), and moral. Answers range from 1 (Not at all) to 5 (A great deal); total 5 to 25. No clinical cutoff has been published, so use it to guide exploration rather than as pass or fail. The item about feeling abandoned by God connects directly to the core lie "God has abandoned me." Spiritual struggle has been linked to suicidal thoughts beyond depression and anxiety, so high scores call for prompt review. Not yet online: waiting for permission from the authors.

Brief RCOPE (Pargament, Feuille & Burdzy, 2011). Fourteen items measuring positive and negative religious coping with a major life stressor. Use when a specific stressor is central. The negative religious coping items (for example, feeling punished or abandoned by God) often point to false beliefs. Can be repeated later to show change. Not yet online: confirming permission for online scoring.

SAI, Spiritual Assessment Inventory (Hall & Edwards, 2002). Forty-seven items, about 10 to 15 minutes, written from a Judeo-Christian view of the person's relationship with God. Reserve for in-depth work when the client's relationship with God is the main issue. Not yet online: author permission needed.

4. Christian Experiences and Beliefs checklist

Kept separate from the scored screens as a counselor-guided form. The counselor either hands the client a paper copy or tells them how to complete it, and then goes over it with the client in session. Available on the Forms page.

5. Other follow-up forms

The counselor may also assign the Life Events & Beliefs Screening, a checklist for the level or levels most relevant to the client's false beliefs, and any forms needed for the differential diagnosis.
