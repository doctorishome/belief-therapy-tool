This note walks through the 14 steps of care in Belief Counseling, from the first phone call to the belief cards used as homework. The printable flow chart is on the Forms page.

Before the client

Foundations 1: The Basics. Read the public pages first: What Is a Belief?, What Is Belief Counseling?, and What Is Belief Mining? (including Why Beliefs Matter and the six-levels diagram). Dr. Campbell's videos on the basics are in production.

Foundations 2: Counselor Reading. From CBT to Christian CBT (history); Ethical Considerations When Choosing Treatment Modalities; How Beliefs Influence Personality. This section also covers competence and the ethics of making referrals.

Foundations 3: Assessment Skills. Learn these before the first client: the nine-step DSM-5-TR diagnostic process (narrated slides, flow chart, PowerPoint) and the DSM-5-TR diagnosis and coding practice tutorial; the functional analysis tutorial for the presenting problem (being written); and the guide to the screening forms (see "Step 9: Follow-up Forms and Screens").

With the client

1. Telephone intake. A trained intake person takes the call with a blank Telephone Intake form open on the computer. The practice serves adults only. The intake person records basic information (name, email, phone, insurance), asks the caller to describe the general problem, and screens for fit using the counselor's own list of problems the counselor does not treat. Callers who are not a fit are referred to local specialists. The intake person explains that the counselor specializes in helping people who want their Christian faith integrated into treatment. If the caller is a good fit, the intake person schedules the first appointment, sends the link to the intake forms, and asks whether the client will complete the forms online or would prefer paper.

2. Online (or paper) intake. Online is the normal way. Before the first visit the client completes three short forms through the New Client Forms link: About You (contact, emergency contact, and payment information), the Brief History (present concerns, current difficulties, safety questions, treatment and health history, substances, life experiences, and faith and support), and the Life Events & Beliefs Screening. The client then reads and signs the agreements and consent forms on screen: Informed Consent for Treatment, the Confidentiality Agreement, the Notice of Privacy Practices acknowledgment, the Financial Policy, the Telehealth Consent, and the Good Faith Estimate Notice. A Release of Information form and the Client Handbook will be added. The Brief History is used in place of the full Adult History. Paper forms are available for clients without a computer or phone, or who prefer paper. Staff can go through the forms with clients who struggle with reading or writing.

3. Schedule the first visit.

4. Assessment Report begins (built by the AI). The intake information goes into the client's file, and the AI starts the Assessment Report.

5. Review intake information. Before the first interview, the counselor reviews everything, including the AI's report, and forms an initial hypothesis about the diagnosis and an initial hypothesis about toxic beliefs.

6. Initial interview. Belief mining begins.
- Functional analysis of the presenting problem, led by the counselor. The client does not fill out a presenting problem form; the counselor goes through the Presenting Problem Assessment questions with the client.
- Consider safety issues.
- Review the Life Events & Beliefs Screening and the Brief History.

7. Develop hypotheses about the diagnosis and possible false beliefs.

8. Counselor enters the first case note. The counselor dictates the hypotheses and the functional analysis findings into the case note, and the AI adds the interview findings to the Assessment Report. After each case note, the AI suggests questions for the next session: the DSM-5-TR questions still needed for the diagnosis, and questions that help identify core irrational beliefs. The counselor decides which to ask. Steps 6 through 8 repeat after every session.

9. Follow-up tests or assessments. At the end of the session, the counselor checks off the follow-up forms on the Forms to Complete card and sends the client one link (or prints the card for a paper client). Options include the PHQ-9 (depression), GAD-7 (anxiety), the Christian Experiences and Beliefs checklist (counselor-guided), level checklists, and diagnostic forms. If faith is included, the RSS-5 and Brief RCOPE are optional screens completed in the client record in Clinical Companion. The Spiritual Assessment Inventory is not included. See the Knowledge Library note "Step 9: Follow-up Forms and Screens."

10. Diagnosis + Identify. DSM-5-TR diagnosis while identifying the client's false beliefs. Refresher: the nine-step DSM-5-TR diagnostic process from Foundations 3.

11. Treatment plan, built from the diagnosis and the false beliefs.

12. Challenge. Cognitive restructuring with secular and Christian techniques.

13. Replace. False beliefs are replaced with the truth.

14. Belief cards. The public belief cards are used as homework.
