Synopsis

(Godly Counsel: pp. 31–38.)

God’s Anger: The Lord our God is gracious, loving, forgiving, and compassionate. He is slow to anger and rich in love. However, when God is provoked sufficiently, His anger can be fierce. For example, He destroyed the city of Sodom and Gomorrah because of their evil ways, and His anger “burned” against the pharaoh when he would not let the Jewish people leave Egypt.

God is a jealous God, and He gets angry when His people worship idols or other “gods,” and when they forsake Him. If we reject God’s word, as contained in the Bible, and fail to follow His commandments, we should properly fear God’s anger and wrath. Fortunately, if we obey God’s commands, humble ourselves before Him, and seek righteousness, we can shield ourselves from God’s anger.

Christ’s Righteous Anger: Jesus Christ showed anger when He drove the moneychangers out of the temple. He also got angry when people doubted that He could heal a man’s hand, and when the Pharisees were being hypocritical. These were instances of “righteous anger.”

Man’s Righteous Anger/Rebuke: Righteous anger is the only kind of anger that God permits for human beings, and even when we engage in this type of anger, we must be careful that we do not “cross over the line” and sin. Whenever possible, we should rebuke our fellow man frankly, but gently, remembering our own sinful nature and imperfections. We are to correct, rebuke, and encourage “with every form of patient instruction.”

Refrain From Anger: Unfortunately, man’s anger is not always “righteous anger,” and the Bible advises against most forms of anger. In general, God desires that we refrain from anger and keep ourselves under control. Quick-tempered people often do foolish things, and man’s anger doesn’t bring about the righteous life that God desires.

God’s Guidance
Watch What You Say: God wants us to be careful about how we talk to others. Jesus reminds us that all men will have to give an account on the day of judgment for every careless word they have spoken. Reckless words can “pierce like a sword,” and can do significant harm to others.

Control Yourself: When provoked, try to remain calm and avoid shouting. Step back and think about what you are going to say before you say it, and don’t speak in haste. Often, you can calm a quarrel by remaining quiet and being patient. Remember that a “gentle answer” can defuse a situation and stop an argument from getting out of control. In fact, if you control your temper and are patient, your gentle answer can prove to be a major “weapon” against your opponent.

Keep Things In Perspective: Avoid foolish and stupid arguments; they only produce quarrels. People who love to quarrel love sin. Keep things in perspective, and learn to drop things that aren’t really that important. Don’t pay attention to every word that people say and try to “hold your tongue” before speaking; develop good listening skills, and try not to respond without first hearing what the other person has to say.

Don’t Go to Bed Angry: If possible, try to resolve your anger with another person before you go to bed at night. This may require that you forgive the other person, or ask the other person to forgive you. Apparently the devil loves it when we go to bed angry and upset at each other.

Stay Away From Angry People: Anger can be contagious. Scriptures warn us to stay away from people who get angered easily, and avoid making friends with hot-tempered people—so that we don’t learn their ways and become angry people ourselves. Hot-tempered people commit many sins, and get into trouble a lot. If you rescue them from their trouble, you will probably have to do it again and again.

Be Wise and Try to Live in Peace: Finally, if you are wise, you will learn to restrain your words and your temper, and turn anger away whenever possible. Never try to stir up anger. Make every effort to live peacefully with all men. Love each other, as God loves us. Remember that love is patient and kind; it keeps no record of wrongs, and it is not easily angered.
