Synopsis

(Godly Counsel: pp. 91–103.)

Hopeless: One of the triggers for depression is the false belief that your situation is hopeless — a lie contradicted throughout the Bible. God alone is the source of our hope; when Jesus Christ died on the cross and was raised from the dead, he became a living hope for all of us. If you are feeling downcast, put your hope in God’s promise of eternal salvation. Be patient in your afflictions and faithful in prayer; even suffering can help develop character and ultimately lead to hope.

Helpless: If you are feeling helpless or powerless, turn to the Lord and His strength; nothing is too hard for God. Worthless: If you are feeling worthless, consider that you are made in God’s image, and that God loves you more than you can ever know. You don’t have to be rich, successful, or perfect to be worthy in God’s eyes.

Joyless: Christians who are joyless have lost perspective; try meditating on God’s incredible love for you — not only did God send His son to die for us, He sent the Holy Spirit to testify that we are His children. Energyless: People who are depressed often feel weak and energyless; turn to God for help, since He gives strength to the weary. One of the best things you can do to help yourself is to “get up” and “take action” — helping others will serve to get your mind off your own problems.

Guilty: If you have sinned, confess your sin and turn it over to God, knowing it will never be counted against you again; if you have harmed someone, take time to confess your sin to him or her and seek restoration. When you commit a sin you “grieve the Holy Spirit” who lives within you, so it is only natural to feel temporary sorrow and guilt — but that guilt should be short-lived, as you move on to accept the gift of forgiveness that was paid in full by Jesus Christ on the cross.
