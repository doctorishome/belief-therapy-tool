Synopsis

(Godly Counsel: pp. 15–23.)

Alcoholism: People who drink too much alcohol often ruin their own lives and the lives of those around them; their world is full of strife and sorrow. When a person drinks alcohol, it can go down smoothly at first, but in the end it “bites like a snake and stings like a viper.” Getting drunk leads to a life focused on sensual pleasure (debauchery).

People who drink alcohol often brag about how much they can drink. They stay up late drinking and then when they wake up they want to drink again. They ridicule others (mock them) and often get into fights. In the end, alcohol consumption can lead to a life of poverty. Most importantly, people who live a lifestyle of drunkenness and lasciviousness place themselves in danger of losing the biggest prize of all—the kingdom of God. Gluttony: For people who eat too much, their “god” is their stomach; they have their minds set on earthly things. Like the drunkard, gluttons often end up becoming poor because of their overindulgence. The Bible warns us that a companion of gluttons “disgraces his father.”

Drug Addiction: Drug addicts abuse their bodies. Such abuse is offensive to God, because our bodies are God’s temple and the Holy Spirit lives in us. Any one who destroys God’s sacred temple, God will destroy him. Drug addicts are slaves to their drugs and controlled by their sinful nature. Living according to the sinful nature leads to death, but living according to the Spirit leads to life. Those who live by the sinful nature cannot please God. God wants us to purify ourselves from everything that contaminates the body and spirit, “perfecting holiness in the fear of God.”

The men and women of Sodom and Gomorrah and ancient Rome became preoccupied with sex, with a continual lust for more. Both these civilizations perished. As Christians, we are to abstain from sexual immorality; all other sins a man commits are outside his body, but he who sins sexually sins against his own body. We are to learn to control our bodies in a way that is holy and honorable, not in passionate lust like the people who do not know God. We are to “put to death” sexual immorality and impurity and everything that belongs to our earthly nature. We are to get rid of all moral filth. Your body is a “temple of the Holy Spirit,” who is in

just do whatever you want to do with your body. It was bought for a price—Christ’s death on the cross. Therefore, you are to honor God with your body. People with sexual addictions often take advantage of other people to meet their desires; this runs contrary to God’s teaching. We should treat others with “absolute purity,” without even a hint of sexual immorality. God has a dire warning for those who live a lifestyle of unrepentant sexual sin—they will not inherit the kingdom of God. Gambling Addiction: The Bible warns us to keep ourselves free of the love of money and to be content with what we have. People who want to get rich quickly get trapped in their sin and do many foolish and harmful things that end up in “ruin and destruction.”

People who love money never seem to have enough. People who gamble want to “get rich quick,” but the Bible warns us not to wear ourselves out trying to get rich. The person who chases fantasies of being wealthy will end up with “his fill of poverty,” and anyone eager to get rich “will not go unpunished.”

God’s Guidance
People who are “hooked” on the short-term pleasure of addictions find it extremely difficult to break their habits. They become slaves to their sin. Time after time they have tried to resist, but time after time they have given in to temptation. Only God offers an

All addictions represent a battle between what the flesh wants and what the spirit desires. The first step in overcoming addictions is to recognize that you cannot control your addiction on your own— you need God’s help. Remember that God will never let you be tempted beyond what you can bear, and when you are tempted He will always provide a way out, so that you can “stand up” under the temptation. If you are addicted, turn to God. If you confess with your mouth “Jesus is Lord,” and believe in your heart that God raised Him from the dead, you will receive eternal salvation. You will become an

Holy Spirit, the great Counselor. You will no longer be controlled by the sinful nature, but by the Spirit of God, who lives in you. Live one day at a time, and pray each day that God will take away your sinful desire. Completely abandon yourself to God, and seek His will for your life. Fix your eyes on Jesus, the author and perfector of our faith. “Clothe yourselves with the Lord Jesus Christ,” and stop thinking about how to gratify the desires of your addiction. In humility, regularly confess your sins to God, and to your fellow man. Seek out anyone you may have harmed because of your

addiction and ask for forgiveness. Forgive yourself also, for we are all sinners and have fallen short of the glory of God. Remember, God showed his love for us by this: “While we were still sinners, Christ died for us.” Always have hope. Even though you are not strong enough to conquer your addiction by yourself, with God’s help you can. And if the Son of God sets you free, “you will be free indeed.” Finally, once God has delivered you from your addiction, go and tell others who may also be suffering, so that they too may have hope
