Synopsis

(Godly Counsel: pp. 136–139.)

God warns Christians to keep their lives free from the love of money and to be content with what they have; those who chase riches are often led into foolish and harmful desires that plunge them into ruin. People with money can develop a false sense of security, imagining their wealth a “fortified city” — but God sees behind the wall to the true self, often “wretched, pitiful, poor, blind, and naked.” A rich man dies and takes nothing with him; he came into the world naked and leaves naked. Jesus reminds us how hard it is for a rich man to enter heaven — easier for a camel to pass through the eye of a needle.

God’s Guidance
put your hope in God rather than wealth; be rich in good deeds and generosity rather than money; store up treasures in heaven, where no thief can steal them; and above all, seek righteousness rather than riches — “seek first the kingdom of God.”
