Synopsis

(Godly Counsel: pp. 66–69.)

First of all, we should remember that God alone is the ultimate authority for our lives and our actions. We must seek to follow his commands. Any man-made authority (government) is subject to God’s authority. We are citizens of heaven first, and then citizens of this world. As a general rule, God wants us to obey our leaders and submit to their authority. The authorities that exist have been established by God. If you rebel against authority, you are rebelling what God has instituted, and if you do so, you will bring judgment upon yourself. In most instances, if you do what is right and follow the law, there is no reason to fear the government or authorities. Police officers and other authorities are intended to be servants who help and do good. Authorities also exist in order to punish wrongdoers.

We should pay taxes because it is the right thing to do. Jesus Himself reminded people to pay their taxes, and He was careful to pay His own. Under ordinary circumstances, God's overall desire is for us to be subject to rulers and authorities, to do good, and to live peacefully and humbly with all men. However, when God’s law runs contrary to man’s law, then some forms of civil disobedience may be warranted — for example, when the apostles were given a direct command by authorities not to preach in Jesus’ name, under threat of going to jail, Peter and the other apostles with him stated: “We must obey God rather than men.” We know that at least some of the original apostles later on became martyrs and paid with their lives because they obeyed God’s command (to spread the gospel) rather than man’s commands.
