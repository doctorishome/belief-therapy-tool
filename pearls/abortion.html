Synopsis

(Godly Counsel: pp. 9–14.)

Almighty God is the creator of all things, including all human life. We exist, because He exists. A mother’s womb is not a secret place to our heavenly Father; the unborn baby is not hidden

from God’s sight. The creation of human life from the union of egg and sperm is a miraculous process made possible by God’s creative power. Human beings are made in God’s image. From the moment of conception, and throughout the entire nine months of prenatal development, God’s hands form us and mold us according to the purposes He has planned for us in life. Without question, God is actively and intimately involved in the formation of babies from the point of conception until birth.

Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior, went through the process of human development in His mother’s womb, just like all other babies in the history of mankind. Following His miraculous conception by the Holy Spirit, Jesus grew as an unborn baby in Mary’s womb until she gave birth to Him in Bethlehem. From the point of conception onward, Jesus was fully human and fully God. The history of Jesus’ life on earth begins at the point of conception. The word "fetus" does not appear in the Bible. Instead, the unborn baby in the womb is referred to as a "baby," "child," or "infant." These terms imply the existence of a separate human being. For example, when Mary became pregnant with Jesus, she was said to be "with child." Using similar terminology, the prophet Job referred to a stillborn baby who died before birth as an "infant" who never saw the light of day."

While Mary was still pregnant with Jesus, she visited her sister, Elizabeth, who was also pregnant with a son who would be named John the Baptist. When Elizabeth heard Mary's greeting, the baby (John the Baptist) "leaped for joy," in his mother’s womb when he came into the presence of Jesus. The mother’s womb is sacred; God sometimes assigns a very special role to babies while they are still in the womb. For example, before the prophet Jeremiah was born, while he was still in his mother’s womb, he was appointed by God to be a prophet to the nations. The prophet Isaiah also received his calling before he was born—while still being formed by God in his mother’s womb. The Bible condemns murder and the killing of the innocent. The Tenth Commandment proclaims: Thou shall not murder." The Lord hates "hands that shed innocent blood."

God’s Guidance
Abortions are typically "secret" endeavors; but nothing in all creation is hidden from God’s sight. He is aware of all we do and considers whether our actions are good or evil. Without the redemptive sacrifice of Jesus Christ on the cross, God would bring every deed into judgment, including every hidden thing that we have done. Fortunately, if we believe in Jesus and repent of our sins, forgiveness is possible for all sins, even abortion.

When we confess our sins and are forgiven, our sins are never counted against us. They are removed "as far as the east is from the west." Once forgiven, God does not even remember our sins any more. The main theme of the Bible is God’s unfailing love for us, His children, whom He created. He loved us so much he sent His only Son to die for us. Because He first loved us, we are to love one another deeply from the heart. We are to do unto others what we would have others do unto us. These commands are vitally important and relevant when considering the act of abortion.
