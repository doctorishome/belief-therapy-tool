Synopsis

(Godly Counsel: pp. 39–42.)

People who suffer from anxiety and worry tend to focus on the future. They imagine harmful events or catastrophes that may never occur. God’s word tells us that we cannot discover anything about the future. We are to stay in the present and turn all our anxiety over to God because he cares for us. When we give our

anxiety to God, He promises that He will sustain us and that He will never let the righteous man fall. We are to cope with today’s problems, and not worry about what tomorrow may bring. In addition to living in the future, people who are anxious tend to “blow things up out of proportion,” and worry about this or that little thing. God does not want us to fret about the small stuff. Instead, He wants us to focus on Him, and trust Him for all our needs—even for relatively “big” items like food and clothes. God is well aware that we need these things and promises He will provide. In the final analysis, God does not want us to be anxious about anything, whether in the present or in the future, and whether “big” or “small.” When we are worried or afraid, He invites us to pray to Him and present our requests to Him. When we do so, He promises He will provide us with peace—peace that is beyond what we can ever understand or grasp.

God’s Guidance
God is a loving and powerful Father, who watches over all who love Him. He does not want his children to be fearful or afraid. He instructs us to believe in Him and to trust in Him, and He promises that He will watch over us and deliver us from all our fears. He is not a God far away, He is a God nearby. We do not need to fear natural disasters; God is more powerful than earthquakes and sunami’s. And we do not need to fear

what human beings can do to us as we walk on this earth. The worst thing a human being can do is to take our life (they cannot touch our souls). But God has infinitely more power; He can destroy your body and put your soul in hell. Therefore, the only thing we should properly fear is God alone—who has the power to send our bodies and souls into hell for eternity. This type of fear is the beginning of knowledge and wisdom. Finally, as Christians, we should stand firm in our faith and never fear giving our testimony and spreading the gospel. When we became Christians, God did not give us a spirit of fear, but a spirit of power, of love and of self-discipline.
