Synopsis

(Godly Counsel: pp. 43–52.)

Most people are “on fire” for the Lord when they first become Christians—when they are “born again.” However, slowly over time, their enthusiasm can begin to grow cold as they live out their lives in a fallen and sin-filled world.

Sometimes the process of backsliding is largely a “passive” process, whereby the believer gradually drifts away from a Spirit-filled relationship with God. The Bible uses a variety of terms to describe this process:

• Forgetting God
• Heart Turning Away From God
• Forsaking Your First Love
• Turning Away From the Gospel

• Wandering From The Faith
• Becoming Lazy
• Being Complacent
• Being Lukewarm

Other scriptures suggest that backsliding can take the form of a more conscious and “active” role in the decision to turn to evil and disobey God. The end product of this decision can be slavery to sin.

• Turning To Follow Satan
• Becoming Slaves To Sin
• Being Taken Captive By Satan
• Turning to Evil

The Bible also describes the condition of “backsliding” metaphorically, as:

• Salt Losing Its Saltiness
• Being Asleep
• Serving Two Masters
• Grass Without Water
• Seed Sown Among Thorns
• A Dead Branch
• Building A House Without a Firm Foundation
• Being Taken Captive Through Hollow and Deceptive Philosophy

All Christians backslide temporarily at one point or another in their lives; however, remaining in a state of indifference, or sinful separation from God, can have serious outcomes. As time progresses, our hearts may become hardened through backsliding and our consciences may become “seared.”

That’s one reason why God recommends that we examine ourselves to see if we are living fully in the faith. We can undertake this self-evaluation by looking at the fruit that our lives are bearing. Are we reaching others for Christ? Are we helping to change lives? If not, then we are almost certainly backsliding.

In the end, backsliding always has negative and bitter consequences. God wants us to return to Him. He wants us to be alert and self-controlled. He wants us to repent and do the things we did when we first knew Him.

Because God loves us so much, He disciplines His children when they drift away from Him or fall back into sin. Everyone who sins is disciplined. If you are not disciplined, then you are not true children of God. No discipline seems pleasant at the time, but later on it produces a “harvest of righteousness and peace” for those who are trained by it. Bottom line, consider yourself blessed if you are being disciplined for your backsliding.

Be prepared! Our time on earth is short and Christ will be returning soon. We need to have our lives in order when He comes, for He will come “like a thief in the night”—at a time when we do not expect Him. We need to wake up from our slumber and turn back to God, for He is coming soon.

God’s Guidance
Human beings are weak but God is strong. He stands knocking at the door, and wants us to return to Him. Through the power of God, we have been given everything we need to live a life of godliness, and to escape backsliding and the corruption of the world that is caused by evil desires. The Lord knows how to rescue godly men from trials. Repent! And turn back to God. Come back to your senses and stop sinning.

If you think you are standing firm in your faith, be careful you don’t fall. If you don’t stand firm in your faith, you won’t stand at all. Let nothing move you; always give yourself fully to the Lord. Hold on to the confidence and enthusiasm you had when you first knew God.

Don’t swerve to the left or to the right; keep you feet from evil. Sow righteousness. Keep growing spiritually. Make every effort to add to your faith: goodness; knowledge; self-control; perseverance; godliness; brotherly kindness; and love. Aim for perfection; don’t settle for “good enough.” God doesn’t like lukewarm Christians.

Resist the devil and he will flee from you. Just as Satan tempted Jesus when He was weak, he will tempt us when we have our guard down and are distracted by the world. Pray to God for strength and protection from the evil one. In fact, devote yourself to prayer, being watchful that you do not backslide, and thankful that you know our Lord and Savior, Jesus Christ.

Read the Bible on a regular basis, and study the scriptures. Do not neglect God’s word. Hide the word of God in your heart, so that you don’t slip away from Him and fall into sin.

In the end, make every effort to live holy and godly lives. Conduct yourselves in a manner worthy of the gospel of Christ.
