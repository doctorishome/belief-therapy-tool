Spiritual/Religious False Belief #3 · God

“God is disappointed in me.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

A parent who sighed, a teacher who was never pleased, or a church that noticed failures more than faith can teach the heart to expect disappointment. Real sin adds shame. The thought “I let God down, and He is tired of me” can feel like His voice. It is often an old scorekeeper, not the Father who calls you His child.

What Scripture teaches

Scripture does not describe the Father as a parent who has given up on His children.

Zephaniah 3:17The LORD your God is among you; He is mighty to save. He will rejoice over you with gladness; He will quiet you with His love; He will rejoice over you with singing.

In Christ there is no condemnation.

Romans 8:1Therefore, there is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus.

He has a father’s compassion and knows we are dust.

Psalm 103:13–14As a father has compassion on his children, so the LORD has compassion on those who fear Him. For He knows our frame; He is mindful that we are dust.

He loved us while we were still sinners.

Romans 5:8But God proves His love for us in this: While we were still sinners, Christ died for us.

He calls us His children.

1 John 3:1Behold what manner of love the Father has given to us, that we should be called children of God. And that is what we are! The reason the world does not know us is that it did not know Him.

He is pleased with those who fear Him and hope in His love.

Psalm 147:11The LORD is pleased with those who fear Him, who hope in His loving devotion.

He knows you are dust, and He still has compassion. Sin is real, and it is not the same thing as being a disappointment He has finished with.

Also see

Romans 8:15–16For you did not receive a spirit of slavery that returns you to fear, but you received the Spirit of sonship, by whom we cry, ‘Abba! Father!’ The Spirit Himself testifies with our spirit that we are God’s children.

Galatians 4:6–7And because you are sons, God sent the Spirit of His Son into our hearts, crying out, ‘Abba, Father!’ So you are no longer a slave, but a son; and since you are a son, you are also an heir through God.

John 1:12But to all who did receive Him, to those who believed in His name, He gave the right to become children of God.

Isaiah 49:15–16‘Can a woman forget her nursing child, or lack compassion for the son of her womb? Even if she could forget, I will not forget you! Behold, I have inscribed you on the palms of My hands; your walls are ever before Me.’

Lamentations 3:22–23Because of the loving devotion of the LORD we are not consumed, for His mercies never fail. They are new every morning; great is Your faithfulness!

Common self-statements

  • I am a disappointment to God.
  • He expected more, and I failed Him.
  • Other Christians make Him glad. I make Him tired.
  • If He really knew this week, He would stop loving me.
  • I should hide until I have improved.
  • His love is for the people who get it right.

Common logical errors

  • Emotional reasoning: Feeling like a letdown and calling that feeling God’s opinion.
  • Mind reading: Deciding what God thinks of me without asking what He has said.
  • All-or-nothing thinking: If I am not impressive, I must be a disappointment.
  • Discounting the positive: Ignoring the cross, the Father’s compassion, and the name He gives His children.

Challenging questions

  • Whose disappointed voice am I hearing: God’s, or someone from my past?
  • If God is for me, who can condemn me?

    Romans 8:31–32What then shall we say in response to these things? If God is for us, who can be against us? He who did not spare His own Son but gave Him up for us all, how will He not also, along with Him, freely give us all things?

  • What does the Lord do for the people who fear Him here?

    Psalm 149:4For the LORD takes pleasure in His people; He adorns the afflicted with salvation.

  • How does Jesus describe the love He has for His own?

    John 15:9As the Father has loved Me, so have I loved you. Remain in My love.

  • What would I say to a child who thought a good father was disappointed and done?
  • Did God love me before I loved Him?

    1 John 4:10And love consists in this: not that we loved God, but that He loved us and sent His Son as the atoning sacrifice for our sins.

Truth summary

God is not disappointed in me as if He has given up. In Christ there is no condemnation. He has compassion on me, He loved me while I was still a sinner, and He rejoices over His people with love.

    ☐ An everyday event: every time I ______________________________
    ☐ A visual reminder: something I will see often, such as a sticky note on my mirror, my truth as the background picture on my phone, or a note on my car dashboard.
Pick one or two that fit me
  • When I was sure God was disappointed: Each day, write down one moment when a failure felt like proof God was tired of me. Write what happened, what you told yourself, and one more accurate statement.
