Sociocultural False Belief #30 · Appearance and Body Image

“I'll be happy once I look the way I'm supposed to.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

The “when I lose X pounds, get this procedure, or fix this feature” story is persuasive. Each goal postpones happiness, and when it is reached, a new flaw appears. Years can pass waiting for a version of ourselves that never arrives.

What Scripture teaches

Paul writes, “I have learned to be content” in plenty and in need. “Godliness with contentment is great gain.” He urges, “Set your minds on things above, not on earthly things.” Proverbs says, “Charm is deceptive and beauty is fleeting.” Paul also says that our citizenship is in heaven and that Christ “will transform our lowly bodies to be like His glorious body.” Caring for our bodies is good, and wanting to feel healthy is natural, but happiness postponed until a body changes is happiness we will not find.

I know how to live humbly, and I know how to abound. I am accustomed to any and every situation—to being filled and being hungry, to having plenty and having need. I can do all things through Christ who gives me strength (Philippians 4:12–13).

Also see

Set your minds on things above, not on earthly things (Colossians 3:2).

Of course, godliness with contentment is great gain. For we brought nothing into the world, so we cannot carry anything out of it. But if we have food and clothing, we will be content with these (1 Timothy 6:6–8).

But our citizenship is in heaven, and we eagerly await a Savior from there, the Lord Jesus Christ, who, by the power that enables Him to subject all things to Himself, will transform our lowly bodies to be like His glorious body (Philippians 3:20–21).

Charm is deceptive and beauty is fleeting, but a woman who fears the LORD is to be praised (Proverbs 31:30).

Common self-statements

  • Once I look right, my life will start.
  • I cannot enjoy anything until I change.
  • Happy people are attractive.
  • I will date, travel, or be photographed when I look better.
  • Then I will be at peace with myself.
  • I am waiting for the real me.

Common logical errors

  • Magical thinking: Believing a change in appearance will solve my unhappiness.
  • Postponement: Putting life on hold until I look different.
  • All-or-nothing thinking: Believing I am either at the goal or failing.
  • Should statements: Insisting that I should look a certain way.

Challenging questions

  • What have I put on hold until my body changes?
  • Have I ever reached a goal and found lasting happiness?
  • What parts of my life could I enjoy now?
  • What is the difference between caring for health and chasing an image?
  • What does Scripture say about contentment?
  • What is one thing I can do this week that I have been postponing?

Truth summary

Happiness does not wait at the end of a body goal. I can care for my health, learn contentment, and start living now. Contentment is a gift that frees me from the chase.
