Sociocultural False Belief #28 · Appearance and Body Image

“My body determines my worth.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

Fitness culture, medical anxiety, fashion, and sexual objectification tell us that our value is how our bodies look or perform. Illness, injury, aging, and weight changes can then feel like losses of worth, not merely of function.

What Scripture teaches

God created humanity “in His own image,” which is true of every person and every body. Paul writes, “Do you not know that your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit who is in you…? You are not your own; you were bought at a price. Therefore glorify God with your body.” The psalmist says, “All my days were written in Your book.” Paul adds, “Though our outer self is wasting away, yet our inner self is being renewed day by day.” Our bodies are gifts to steward, not measures of our worth.

Do you not know that your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit who is in you, whom you have received from God? You are not your own; you were bought at a price. Therefore glorify God with your body (1 Corinthians 6:19–20).

Also see

So God created man in His own image; in the image of God He created him; male and female He created them (Genesis 1:27).

My frame was not hidden from You when I was made in secret, when I was woven together in the depths of the earth. Your eyes saw my unformed body; all my days were written in Your book and ordained for me before one of them came to be (Psalm 139:15–16).

Therefore we do not lose heart. Though our outer self is wasting away, yet our inner self is being renewed day by day (2 Corinthians 4:16).

And even the very hairs of your head are all numbered (Matthew 10:30).

Common self-statements

  • I am only as valuable as my body looks or performs.
  • If my body fails, I fail.
  • My body is the problem.
  • Aging means losing value.
  • I am worth more when I am thin or fit.
  • I hate my body.

Common logical errors

  • Labeling: Equating myself with my body’s condition.
  • All-or-nothing thinking: Seeing my body as perfect or worthless.
  • Perfectionism: Demanding an ideal I cannot sustain.
  • Emotional reasoning: Feeling dissatisfied and concluding that my worth is low.

Challenging questions

  • How have I linked my worth to my body, and where did I learn it?
  • What can my body do that I overlook?
  • What does it mean that I am made in God’s image?
  • What does it mean to steward a body, not worship or punish it?
  • How would I treat a friend’s body or illness?
  • What is one kind thing I can do for my body?

Truth summary

My worth comes from being made in God’s image and bought at a price, not from how my body looks or performs. I can steward my body with care and thanks, whatever its condition.

  • Worth beyond the body: Each day, name one thing your body did for you and one thing that is true of you apart from your body.
