Sociocultural False Belief #9 · Race, Ethnicity and Language

“Everyone from that group is the same.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

Stereotypes are learned early: from family jokes, news coverage, a bad experience, or a neighborhood’s attitudes. Our minds sort people into categories to save effort, and fear or hurt can harden those categories. The result is judging individuals by a label.

What Scripture teaches

God told Samuel, “Man sees the outward appearance, but the LORD sees the heart,” and Peter learned that God shows no favoritism. James writes, “do not show favoritism.” Genesis teaches, “God created man in His own image… male and female He created them.” Jesus welcomed a Samaritan woman and held up a Samaritan as the hero of a story; the Samaritan “had compassion.” Scripture insists that every person is an individual made in God’s image. Labels flatten what God made unique.

My brothers, as you hold out your faith in our glorious Lord Jesus Christ, do not show favoritism (James 2:1).

Also see

So God created man in His own image; in the image of God He created him; male and female He created them (Genesis 1:27).

But when a Samaritan on a journey came upon him, he looked at him and had compassion (Luke 10:33).

Here there is no Greek or Jew, circumcised or uncircumcised, barbarian, Scythian, slave, or free, but Christ is all and is in all (Colossians 3:11).

From one man He made every nation of men, that they should inhabit the whole earth; and He determined their appointed times and the boundaries of their lands (Acts 17:26).

Common self-statements

  • They are all alike.
  • You can’t trust any of them.
  • I know what those people are like.
  • One bad experience tells me everything.
  • They are all the same.
  • I do not need to know them.

Common logical errors

  • Overgeneralizing: Treating one person or experience as representing a group.
  • Labeling: Reducing individuals to a category.
  • Confirmation bias: Noticing only what fits the stereotype.
  • Mind reading: Assuming I know what an individual thinks.

Challenging questions

  • Where did I first learn this idea about this group?
  • Do I know anyone from this group personally? What are they like?
  • Would I want someone to judge me by a label?
  • What exceptions have I noticed, and what does that suggest?
  • What does it mean that every person is made in God’s image?
  • How could I get to know one person as an individual?

Truth summary

Every person is made in God’s image and is an individual. Labels hide the person God sees. I can examine my assumptions, listen, and treat people one by one.
