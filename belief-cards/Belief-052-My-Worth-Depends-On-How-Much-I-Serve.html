Spiritual/Religious False Belief #52 · Religious Upbringing & Training

“My worth depends on how much I serve.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

Being praised mainly for being helpful or useful as a child. A church culture that celebrated the busiest volunteers. Feeling valuable only when needed. Trouble saying no. Guilt when resting or when others serve you.

What Scripture teaches

Your worth comes from God’s love, not your usefulness. God tells His people that they are precious and honored in His sight, and that He loves them. When Martha was busy serving and Mary sat at Jesus’ feet, Jesus said Mary had chosen the good portion. Service matters: we are created in Christ to do good works. But we serve because we are loved and valuable, not to become loved and valuable.

Because you are precious and honored in My sight, and because I love you, I will give men in exchange for you and nations in place of your life (Isaiah 43:4).

Also see

‘Martha, Martha,’ the Lord replied, ‘you are worried and upset about many things. But only one thing is necessary. Mary has chosen the good portion, and it will not be taken away from her.’ (Luke 10:41–42).

For we are God’s workmanship, created in Christ Jesus to do good works, which God prepared in advance as our way of life (Ephesians 2:10).

Come to Me, all you who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest. Take My yoke upon you and learn from Me; for I am gentle and humble in heart, and you will find rest for your souls. For My yoke is easy and My burden is light (Matthew 11:28–30).

And He said to them, ‘Come with Me privately to a solitary place, and let us rest for a while.’ For many people were coming and going, and they did not even have time to eat (Mark 6:31).

Common self-statements

  • I’m only valuable when I’m helping.
  • If I stop serving, no one will want me.
  • I have to say yes to every request.
  • Rest is selfish.
  • God loves people who do more for Him.
  • My busyness proves my faithfulness.

Common logical errors

  • Conditional worth: Believing your value depends on what you do.
  • Should statements: “I should always be available to serve.”
  • Labeling: Calling yourself “useless” when you are not serving.
  • All-or-nothing thinking: Believing you must serve constantly or be worthless.

Challenging questions

  • What does God say about my worth in Isaiah 43:4?
  • Why did Jesus say Mary chose the better portion?
  • Am I serving from love or from fear of being worthless?
  • Did Jesus ever rest or tell His disciples to rest?
  • Would I still be loved by God if I could never serve again?
  • Who taught me that my worth comes from being useful?
  • What would healthy service look like for me?

Truth summary

My worth does not depend on how much I serve. I am precious and honored in God’s sight because He loves me. Jesus values time with Him more than busyness for Him. I can serve out of love and gratitude, and I can also rest, say no, and let others serve me.

  • Being, not doing: Each day, write one way God showed He loves me that had nothing to do with what I did.
