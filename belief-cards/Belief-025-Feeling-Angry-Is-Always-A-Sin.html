Spiritual/Religious False Belief #25 · The Soul: Emotions, Body & Mind

“Feeling angry is always a sin.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

A home where anger was frightening, violent, or forbidden. Being punished for showing any frustration as a child. Church teaching that treated all anger as sinful. Seeing anger only expressed in harmful ways. Trying to be “nice” at all costs, then feeling guilty when anger builds anyway.

What Scripture teaches

Anger is an emotion God gave us, and it can be a right response to wrong. God Himself is slow to anger, but not without it. Jesus looked around with anger at hardness of heart. Scripture says, “Be angry, yet do not sin.” The question is not whether you feel anger, but what you do with it. Anger becomes sin when it is held onto, turns into bitterness, or is expressed in harmful ways.

‘Be angry, yet do not sin.’ Do not let the sun set upon your anger, and do not give the devil a foothold (Ephesians 4:26–27).

Also see

Jesus looked around at them with anger and sorrow at their hardness of heart. Then He said to the man, ‘Stretch out your hand.’ So he stretched it out, and it was restored (Mark 3:5).

Be angry, yet do not sin; on your bed, search your heart and be still (Psalm 4:4).

My beloved brothers, understand this: Everyone should be quick to listen, slow to speak, and slow to anger, for man’s anger does not bring about the righteousness that God desires (James 1:19–20).

The LORD is compassionate and gracious, slow to anger, abounding in loving devotion (Psalm 103:8).

Common self-statements

  • Good Christians never get angry.
  • I’m sinning just by feeling this.
  • I have to stuff my anger down.
  • If I’m angry, God is angry with me.
  • Anger always hurts people.
  • I can’t admit I’m upset.

Common logical errors

  • All-or-nothing thinking: Believing anger is either completely absent or completely sinful.
  • Confusing feelings with actions: Treating the feeling of anger as the same as acting in anger.
  • Should statements: “I should never feel angry.”
  • Overgeneralization: Assuming all anger is like the harmful anger you saw growing up.
  • Emotional suppression: Pushing anger down until it comes out in other ways.

Challenging questions

  • Did Jesus ever feel angry? Did He sin?
  • What does “Be angry, yet do not sin” tell me?
  • Is the problem the feeling, or what I do with it?
  • Where did I learn that all anger is sinful?
  • What happens when I stuff my anger instead of dealing with it?
  • Is there a wrong here that anger is pointing me to?
  • How can I express anger in a way that honors God?

Truth summary

Feeling angry is not always a sin. Anger is a God-given emotion that can point to real wrongs. Jesus Himself was angry at hardness of heart. I can feel anger without sinning by admitting it, bringing it to God, dealing with it quickly, and expressing it in ways that do not harm others.

My action step this week

Do one thing that tests the lie in real life.

  • Next time you feel angry, say calmly, “I feel angry because ___,” to yourself or a safe person, instead of hiding it.
  • Before bedtime, write down anything you are angry about and pray about it, so the sun does not set on it.
  • Read Mark 3:1–6, and write what Jesus was angry about and what He did with it.
  • Take a ten-minute walk when anger rises, then decide what to say or do.
