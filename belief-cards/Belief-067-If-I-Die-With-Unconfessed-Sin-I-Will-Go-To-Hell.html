Spiritual/Religious False Belief #67 · Hell

“If I die with an unconfessed sin, I will go to hell.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

Teaching that forgiveness only covers sins you specifically confess. Fear of dying suddenly before you can confess. A sensitive conscience that keeps searching for unconfessed sins. Stories meant to frighten people into confession. Worry about loved ones who died suddenly.

What Scripture teaches

Confession is good and important; it restores closeness with God. But a believer’s salvation does not depend on confessing every sin before death. No one can even discern all their own errors; David prayed, “Cleanse me from my hidden faults.” When you trusted Christ, God forgave all your trespasses and canceled the debt against you, nailing it to the cross. By one offering, Christ has made perfect forever those who are being sanctified.

When you were dead in your trespasses and in the uncircumcision of your sinful nature, God made you alive with Christ. He forgave us all our trespasses, having canceled the debt ascribed to us in the decrees that stood against us. He took it away, nailing it to the cross! (Colossians 2:13–14).

Also see

Who can discern his own errors? Cleanse me from my hidden faults (Psalm 19:12).

Because by a single offering He has made perfect for all time those who are being sanctified (Hebrews 10:14).

Therefore, there is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus (Romans 8:1).

But if we walk in the light as He is in the light, we have fellowship with one another, and the blood of Jesus His Son cleanses us from all sin (1 John 1:7).

Common self-statements

  • I have to confess every sin or I’ll be lost.
  • What if I forget one?
  • If I die suddenly, I might go to hell.
  • I can never relax until I’ve confessed everything.
  • My salvation depends on my confession list.
  • One missed sin could cost me heaven.

Common logical errors

  • Catastrophizing: Imagining dying at the wrong moment and being lost.
  • Scrupulosity: Searching endlessly for sins to confess.
  • Discounting Christ’s work: Believing forgiveness depends on your perfect confession.
  • Perfectionism: Believing your confession must be complete to count.

Challenging questions

  • What did God do with all my trespasses when I came to Christ?
  • Can anyone know all their own sins, according to Psalm 19:12?
  • What did Christ’s one offering accomplish for those being sanctified?
  • Is my salvation based on my confession list or on Christ’s cross?
  • Why do I confess: to stay saved or to stay close to God?
  • Does checking make me more peaceful or more fearful?
  • What did the thief on the cross have time to confess?

Truth summary

I will not go to hell because I die with an unconfessed sin. When I trusted Christ, God forgave all my trespasses and nailed my debt to the cross. Christ’s one offering has made me perfect forever. I confess my sins to stay close to God, not to stay saved. My salvation rests on Jesus, not on a perfect list.
