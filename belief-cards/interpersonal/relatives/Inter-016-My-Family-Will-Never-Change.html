Interpersonal False Belief #16 · Relatives

“My family will never change.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

Family patterns can feel carved in stone: the same arguments, the same favorites, the same silence about hard things. After years of hoping and trying, it is natural to conclude that nothing will ever be different. Disappointment can turn into resignation or into a quiet bitterness.

What Scripture teaches

Jesus said, “With man this is impossible, but with God all things are possible.” God promises, “I will give you a new heart and put a new spirit within you.” In Scripture, God changed the hearts of people who seemed hopeless, as in the story of the prodigal son. Proverbs says, “The king’s heart is a waterway in the hand of the LORD; He directs it where He pleases.” At the same time, we are told, “If it is possible on your part, live at peace with everyone.” We cannot force change, but we can pray, do our part, and keep our own hearts from hardening.

Common self-statements

  • They are who they are.
  • Nothing I do makes any difference.
  • Every holiday is the same.
  • They will never see the truth.
  • It is pointless to try again.
  • I have to accept it forever.

Common logical errors

  • Fortune telling: Predicting that no change will ever occur.
  • All-or-nothing thinking: Believing change is either total or impossible.
  • Mental filter: Focusing on what has not changed and missing small shifts.
  • Emotional reasoning: Feeling hopeless and concluding it is hopeless.

Challenging questions

  • What exactly do I want my family to change?
  • Has anything shifted over the years, even a little?
  • What part of this is mine to change, such as my responses or expectations?
  • What can I do differently while I pray for them?
  • What does Scripture say about God’s power to change hearts?
  • How can I protect my own peace?

Truth summary

I cannot make my family change, but I do not have to give up hope. God can change hearts, and I can change my own responses. I can pray, set healthy limits, and live well whether or not they change.

My action step this week

Do one thing that tests the lie in real life.

Choose steps you can do safely. If there is violence, coercion, or fear of retaliation, seek private professional support before trying anything with the other person. In an emergency call 911.

  • Name what you want. Write the specific change you hope for.
  • Divide the control. Write what is yours to change and what is not.
  • Pray by name. Pray for each family member for a week.
  • Adjust your expectations. Decide how you will respond next time without waiting for change.
