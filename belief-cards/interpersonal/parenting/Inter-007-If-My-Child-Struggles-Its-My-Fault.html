Interpersonal False Belief #7 · Parenting

“If my child struggles, it's my fault.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

Parents often feel responsible for everything that happens to their children. When a child struggles with anxiety, school, friends, behavior, or faith, we search our own parenting for the cause. Well-meaning advice and a misreading of verses about training children can add to the weight.

What Scripture teaches

Scripture says, “A son will not bear the iniquity of his father, and a father will not bear the iniquity of his son.” “Each of us will give an account of himself to God.” Children are individuals with their own will, temperament, and choices. Proverbs says, “Train up a child in the way he should go, and when he is old he will not depart from it.” That is a general principle of wise living, not a guarantee, and not a measure of a parent’s worth. The father in Jesus’ story of the prodigal son did nothing wrong, and yet his son left. When his son came back, “his father saw him and was filled with compassion. He ran to his son, embraced him, and kissed him.”

The soul who sins is the one who will die. A son will not bear the iniquity of his father, and a father will not bear the iniquity of his son. The righteousness of the righteous man will fall upon him, and the wickedness of the wicked man will fall upon him (Ezekiel 18:20).

Common self-statements

  • I must have caused this.
  • If I had been a better parent, they would not struggle.
  • It is all because of what I did.
  • I should have seen this coming.
  • Other parents’ kids are fine.
  • It is my job to fix everything.

Common logical errors

  • Personalization: Taking responsibility for things beyond my control.
  • Hindsight bias: Judging past choices by what I know now.
  • Mind reading: Assuming my child blames me.
  • Magical thinking: Believing good parenting guarantees a good outcome.

Challenging questions

  • What factors beyond me contribute, such as temperament, friends, biology, or choices?
  • Has my child made decisions that are their own?
  • What real mistakes, if any, can I own without taking all the blame?
  • What does the father in the prodigal son story teach me?
  • What does my child need from me now?
  • What can I trust God with?

Truth summary

I influence my children, but I do not control them. Some of what they face is not my fault, and some may be mine to own and repair. I can love them, take responsibility for what is mine, and trust God with the rest.

My action step this week

Do one thing that tests the lie in real life.

Choose steps you can do safely. If there is violence, coercion, or fear of retaliation, seek private professional support before trying anything with the other person. In an emergency call 911.

  • Make two columns. List what is yours to own and what is not.

  • Be present. Spend one-on-one time with your child without fixing.
