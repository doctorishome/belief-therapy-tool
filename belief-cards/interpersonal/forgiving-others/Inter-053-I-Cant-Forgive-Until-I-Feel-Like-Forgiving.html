Interpersonal False Belief #53 · Forgiving Others

“I can't forgive until I feel like forgiving.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

We assume forgiveness is an emotion, a warm feeling that arrives on its own. When the pain stays, we conclude we have not forgiven and may feel stuck or guilty. Waiting for the feeling can delay healing for years.

What Scripture teaches

Paul commands, “Bear with one another and forgive any complaint you may have against someone else. Forgive as the Lord forgave you.” Jesus said, “When you stand to pray, if you hold anything against another, forgive it.” Forgiveness is spoken of as a decision and an action. Feelings often follow, sometimes slowly. Jesus tells us to “love your enemies, do good to those who hate you, bless those who curse you, pray for those who mistreat you,” which are acts we can do before the feeling arrives. David prayed, “Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a right spirit within me.” We can ask God to change our hearts as we choose obedience.

Bear with one another and forgive any complaint you may have against someone else. Forgive as the Lord forgave you (Colossians 3:13).

But to those of you who will listen, I say: Love your enemies, do good to those who hate you, bless those who curse you, pray for those who mistreat you (Luke 6:27–28).

Common self-statements

  • I do not feel forgiving, so I cannot.
  • If it were real forgiveness, the pain would be gone.
  • I am still angry, so I have not forgiven.
  • I am waiting until my heart is ready.
  • Forcing it would be fake.
  • I cannot choose how I feel.

Common logical errors

  • Emotional reasoning: Treating feelings as the measure of forgiveness.
  • All-or-nothing thinking: Believing forgiveness is either complete or absent.
  • Should statements: Insisting that I should feel peaceful first.
  • Perfectionism: Expecting forgiveness to be complete in one step.

Challenging questions

  • What is the difference between a decision to forgive and a feeling of forgiveness?
  • What small action could I take that is consistent with forgiveness?
  • Has a feeling ever followed a choice in other areas of my life?
  • What am I waiting for before I start?
  • How can I ask God to change my heart?
  • Who can pray with me?

Truth summary

Forgiveness begins as a decision, with feelings following over time. I can choose to release the debt, pray for the person, and ask God to change my heart. I do not have to wait for a feeling.
