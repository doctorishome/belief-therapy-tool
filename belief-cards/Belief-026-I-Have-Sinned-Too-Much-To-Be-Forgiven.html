Spiritual/Religious False Belief #26 · Sin

“I have sinned too much to be forgiven.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

Years of the same struggle, with many failed promises to change. A long list of regrets that seems to grow every day. Being told “You should know better by now.” Picturing God keeping a running tally of every wrong. A parent or authority whose patience ran out quickly.

What Scripture teaches

God does not keep a tally that eventually runs out. If He kept track of iniquities, no one could stand, but with Him there is forgiveness. His mercies are new every morning. Jesus told Peter to forgive not seven times, but seventy-seven times, showing the heart of God. When the prodigal son came home after wasting everything, his father ran to him and embraced him. The amount of your sin is never greater than the mercy of God.

If You, O LORD, kept track of iniquities, then who, O Lord, could stand? But with You there is forgiveness, so that You may be feared (Psalm 130:3–4).

Also see

Because of the loving devotion of the LORD we are not consumed, for His mercies never fail. They are new every morning; great is Your faithfulness! (Lamentations 3:22–23).

So he got up and went to his father. But while he was still in the distance, his father saw him and was filled with compassion. He ran to his son, embraced him, and kissed him (Luke 15:20).

For You, O Lord, are kind and forgiving, rich in loving devotion to all who call on You (Psalm 86:5).

Let the wicked man forsake his own way and the unrighteous man his own thoughts; let him return to the LORD, that He may have compassion, and to our God, for He will freely pardon (Isaiah 55:7).

Common self-statements

  • I’ve used up all my chances.
  • God must be tired of forgiving me.
  • I keep failing, so there’s no hope.
  • There’s a limit, and I’ve reached it.
  • I’ve sinned too many times to come back.
  • God forgives others, but my list is too long.

Common logical errors

  • Projection: Picturing God’s patience running out like a tired human parent’s.
  • Magnification: Making the number of your sins look bigger than God’s mercy.
  • Fortune telling: Predicting that God will refuse you next time.
  • Emotional reasoning: Concluding “I feel hopeless, so I must be beyond help.”
  • Disqualifying the positive: Dismissing the promises that His mercies are new every morning.

Challenging questions

  • If God kept a list, could anyone stand before Him?
  • What does it mean that His mercies are new every morning?
  • How did the father treat the son who wasted everything?
  • If Jesus told us to forgive seventy-seven times, what does that say about God?
  • Who taught me that forgiveness has a limit?
  • Is my real problem too many sins, or not believing God’s mercy?
  • What would I do differently today if I believed I am forgiven?

Truth summary

I have not sinned too much to be forgiven. God does not keep a tally that runs out. With Him there is forgiveness, and His mercies are new every morning. Like the father in the story of the prodigal son, God runs to meet me when I turn back to Him. His mercy is greater than all my sin.

My practice plan

Everyone does these
  • Make my Truth Card (below). Carry it and read it at least three times a day.
  • Memorize my Scripture from Step 4.
  • Set reminders to silently repeat my truth. Check one or more: ☐ A buzz: my watch or phone vibrates every ______ minutes
    ☐ An everyday event: every time I ______________________________
    ☐ A visual reminder: something I will see often, such as a sticky note on my mirror, my truth as the background picture on my phone, or a note on my car dashboard.
Pick one or two that fit me
  • New mercies journal: Each morning, write: “His mercies are new this morning,” and one thing I am starting fresh today.
  • My name in Scripture: Rewrite my verse with my name in it and read it aloud each morning. For example, Lamentations 3:22–23 becomes: “Because of the loving devotion of the LORD, Sarah is not consumed, for His mercies never fail. They are new every morning; great is Your faithfulness!”
  • Gratitude journal: Each night, write three things I thank God for.
  • Truth recording: Record my own voice reading my Truth Card and verse, and listen in the car or on a walk.
  • Reading: The “Mercy” topic in Pearls.
  • Listening: The Overcoming Depression audio download.
