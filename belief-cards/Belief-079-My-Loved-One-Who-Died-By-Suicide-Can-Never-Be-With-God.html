Spiritual/Religious False Belief #79 · Death & Grief

“My loved one who died by suicide can never be with God.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

Teaching that suicide is an unforgivable sin. Fear that because there was no chance to repent, salvation was lost. Painful comments from others after the loss. Deep grief mixed with confusion, anger, and guilt. Not knowing what Scripture actually says.

What Scripture teaches

Suicide is a deep tragedy, and grief after it is especially heavy. But Scripture does not name it as an unforgivable sin. A believer’s salvation rests on Christ’s finished work, not on a final act or final confession. Nothing in all creation can separate those in Christ from the love of God. Many who die by suicide are suffering from illness and overwhelming pain. We cannot see a person’s heart, but we can trust the Judge of all the earth, who is just and full of mercy.

For I am convinced that neither death nor life, neither angels nor principalities, neither the present nor the future, nor any powers, neither height nor depth, nor anything else in all creation, will be able to separate us from the love of God that is in Christ Jesus our Lord (Romans 8:38–39).

Also see

I give them eternal life, and they will never perish. No one can snatch them out of My hand. My Father who has given them to Me is greater than all. No one can snatch them out of My Father’s hand (John 10:28–29).

Far be it from You to do such a thing—to kill the righteous with the wicked, so that the righteous and the wicked are treated alike. Far be it from You! Will not the Judge of all the earth do what is right? (Genesis 18:25).

The LORD is near to the brokenhearted; He saves the contrite in spirit (Psalm 34:18).

He heals the brokenhearted and binds up their wounds (Psalm 147:3).

Common self-statements

  • They can’t be in heaven because of how they died.
  • Suicide is the one sin God won’t forgive.
  • They didn’t have time to repent, so they’re lost.
  • I’ll never see them again.
  • I should have stopped them.
  • God rejected them in their worst moment.

Common logical errors

  • Overgeneralization: Treating one act as the whole story of a person’s relationship with God.
  • Mind reading: Assuming you know your loved one’s final standing with God.
  • Personalization: Blaming yourself for another person’s choice in unbearable pain.
  • Accepting teaching without checking: Believing suicide is unforgivable without biblical basis.

Challenging questions

  • Does Scripture call suicide an unforgivable sin?
  • Does salvation rest on a final confession or on Christ’s work?
  • What does Romans 8:38–39 say can separate believers from God’s love?
  • Could illness and pain have played a part in what happened?
  • Do I know everything that was in my loved one’s heart?
  • Can I trust the Judge of all the earth to be just and merciful?
  • Am I carrying blame that is not mine to carry?

Truth summary

Suicide is a tragedy, but Scripture does not teach that it is unforgivable. Salvation rests on Christ’s finished work, and nothing in all creation can separate those in Christ from God’s love. I cannot see my loved one’s heart, but I can trust a just and merciful God with them. I do not have to carry blame for their choice.
