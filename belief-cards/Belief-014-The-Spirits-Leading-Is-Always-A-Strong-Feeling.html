Spiritual/Religious False Belief #14 · Holy Spirit

“The Spirit’s leading always comes as a strong feeling.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

Hearing people say “God told me” or “I felt led” about every choice. A church that put more weight on impressions than on Scripture and wise counsel. Waiting for a feeling before making decisions, and freezing when it never came. Mistaking anxiety, excitement, or fear for God’s voice. Times when a strong feeling led you in a bad direction.

What Scripture teaches

God guides His people, but not always through strong feelings. Elijah found that the LORD was not in the wind, the earthquake, or the fire, but came in a still, small voice. The Spirit leads through Scripture, a renewed mind, wise counsel, circumstances, and sometimes quiet impressions. Feelings can be helpful, but they must be tested. God gives wisdom generously to those who ask.

Then the LORD said, ‘Go out and stand on the mountain before the LORD. Behold, the LORD is about to pass by.’ And a great and mighty wind tore into the mountains and shattered the rocks before the LORD, but the LORD was not in the wind. After the wind there was an earthquake, but the LORD was not in the earthquake. After the earthquake there was a fire, but the LORD was not in the fire. And after the fire came a still, small voice (1 Kings 19:11–12).

Also see

Do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind. Then you will be able to test and approve what is the good, pleasing, and perfect will of God (Romans 12:2).

Trust in the LORD with all your heart, and lean not on your own understanding; in all your ways acknowledge Him, and He will make your paths straight (Proverbs 3:5–6).

Now if any of you lacks wisdom, he should ask God, who gives generously to all without finding fault, and it will be given to him (James 1:5).

Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits to see whether they are from God. For many false prophets have gone out into the world (1 John 4:1).

Common self-statements

  • I can’t decide until I feel sure.
  • If I don’t feel it, God isn’t leading me.
  • That strong feeling must be from God.
  • I must have missed God because I felt nothing.
  • Other people hear from God; I don’t.
  • My anxiety must be the Spirit warning me.

Common logical errors

  • Emotional reasoning: Treating a strong feeling as automatic proof of God’s will.
  • Mind reading: Assuming you know God is silent because you do not feel Him.
  • Overgeneralization: Turning “God sometimes guides through impressions” into “He always does.”
  • Discounting the positive: Overlooking Scripture, wisdom, and counsel as real ways God leads.
  • Should statements: “I should feel a clear sign before every choice.”

Challenging questions

  • Where was the LORD in Elijah’s story: the fire or the still, small voice?
  • What does Scripture already say about this choice?
  • Have strong feelings ever led me wrong before?
  • Could this feeling be anxiety, excitement, or fear rather than the Spirit?
  • Have I asked God for wisdom and sought wise counsel?
  • Does the Bible tell me to test what I feel?
  • If I make a wise choice in faith, will God abandon me?

Truth summary

The Holy Spirit leads me, but not only through strong feelings. He guides through His Word, a renewed mind, wise counsel, and quiet nudges. I can test my feelings instead of obeying them blindly. When I ask God for wisdom, He gives it generously, and I can move forward in faith.

My action step this week

Do one thing that tests the lie in real life.

  • Make one small decision this week using Scripture, prayer, and common sense, without waiting for a strong feeling. Note the result.
  • Write down a strong feeling about a choice, then wait 24 hours and pray before acting. See if the feeling holds.
  • Ask two wise Christians for counsel on a current decision, and compare their advice with your feelings.
  • Read 1 Kings 19:1–18 slowly, and write how God met Elijah when he was exhausted and afraid.
