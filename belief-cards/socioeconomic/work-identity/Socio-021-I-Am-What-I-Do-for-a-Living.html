Socioeconomic False Belief #21 · Work and Identity

“I am what I do for a living.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

“What do you do?” is often the first question we are asked, and the answer can quietly become who we are. A family that prized a certain career, a profession with long training, or a parent whose identity was their job can teach us to merge the two. This works while the job is going well. When the role changes, ends, or disappoints, the person underneath can feel lost.

What Scripture teaches

Scripture honors work. God worked in creation, and Paul tells us to work with our whole being, as for the Lord. But Scripture places identity before work. We are God’s workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, and the works come from who we are, not the other way around. We are made in God’s image and called by Him. A job is one place where we serve, but it is not the name God calls us by.

For we are God’s workmanship, created in Christ Jesus to do good works, which God prepared in advance as our way of life (Ephesians 2:10).

Also see

So God created man in His own image; in the image of God He created him; male and female He created them (Genesis 1:27).

Whatever you do, work at it with your whole being, for the Lord and not for men (Colossians 3:23).

But you are a chosen people, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for God’s own possession, to proclaim the virtues of Him who called you out of darkness into His marvelous light (1 Peter 2:9).

‘Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I set you apart and appointed you as a prophet to the nations.’ (Jeremiah 1:5).

Common self-statements

  • If I am not my job, I do not know who I am.
  • My title is my identity.
  • When people ask what I do, that is who I am.
  • If my work is ordinary, so am I.
  • Without my role, I disappear.
  • I only matter when I am working.

Common logical errors

  • Labeling: Reducing myself to a title.
  • All-or-nothing thinking: Believing that my identity is either my job or nothing.
  • Mental filter: Seeing only the work part of who I am.
  • Emotional reasoning: Feeling empty when work changes and concluding that I am empty.

Challenging questions

  • If someone asked who I am without using my job, what would I say?
  • What roles, relationships, and values make up my identity besides work?
  • What does God call me, and when did He call me that?
  • How did my family treat work and identity?
  • What do I lose if I let work define me?
  • What qualities would remain if my job changed tomorrow?

Truth summary

My work is a good thing I do, not the whole of who I am. I was made in God’s image and made His workmanship in Christ before I did any job. I can do my work well and hold my title loosely.
