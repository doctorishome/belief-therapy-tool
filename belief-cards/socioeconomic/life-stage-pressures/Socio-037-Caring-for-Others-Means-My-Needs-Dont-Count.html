Socioeconomic False Belief #37 · Life-Stage Pressures

“Caring for others means my needs don't count.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

Parents, caregivers for aging relatives, and people in helping professions often learn to put themselves last. Family messages like “Don’t be selfish” or religious teaching that confuses sacrifice with self-erasure can add to it. Over time, we may stop noticing our own needs, grow tired and resentful, and still feel guilty for wanting anything.

What Scripture teaches

Jesus said the second great commandment is to love our neighbor as ourselves, which assumes that we also care for ourselves. Paul says no one ever hated his own body, but nourishes and cherishes it. Paul also says to look not only to our own interests, but also to the interests of others, which means both. When Elijah was exhausted, God let him sleep and sent an angel to say, “Get up and eat.” Caring for ourselves is not selfish. It is how we keep loving others.

And the second is like it: ‘Love your neighbor as yourself.’ (Matthew 22:39).

Common self-statements

  • My needs are not important.
  • Taking time for myself is selfish.
  • Everyone else comes first.
  • If I stop, everything will fall apart.
  • I should not need anything.
  • Good people do not have needs.

Common logical errors

  • Should statements: Insisting that I must always put others first.
  • All-or-nothing thinking: Believing that I must either serve others or be selfish.
  • Emotional reasoning: Feeling guilty and concluding that I am wrong to rest.
  • Magnification: Treating a short break as a failure of love.

Challenging questions

  • Where did I learn that my needs do not count?
  • What would happen to the people I love if I became exhausted or ill?
  • What does “love your neighbor as yourself” say about how I treat myself?
  • What do I need most right now?
  • Who could share some of the load?
  • What is one small way I can care for myself this week?

Truth summary

My needs count to God, and caring for myself helps me keep loving others. I can care for the people in my life without erasing myself and can ask for help without guilt.
