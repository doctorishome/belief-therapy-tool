Spiritual/Religious False Belief #6 · Christ

“I need a priest, a saint, or Mary to bring my prayers to God.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

Many Christians, including Catholic and Orthodox believers, have been taught with real reverence to ask Mary, a saint, or a priest to carry their prayers. That teaching often comes from love for the church and from a humble fear of approaching God on our own. Pastors and friends may pray with us, and that is a gift. Mary is honored in Scripture. The belief that God will not hear us unless a human or a saint stands in between is a different claim.

What Scripture teaches

Scripture honors Mary and welcomes the prayers of the church. It also names one mediator.

1 Timothy 2:5For there is one God, and there is one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus.

Jesus is our great high priest, so we may come to God ourselves.

Hebrews 4:14–16Therefore, since we have a great high priest who has passed through the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, let us hold firmly to what we profess. For we do not have a high priest who is unable to sympathize with our weaknesses, but we have one who was tempted in every way that we are, yet was without sin. Let us then approach the throne of grace with confidence, so that we may receive mercy and find grace to help us in our time of need.

He lives to intercede for those who draw near through Him.

Hebrews 7:25Therefore He is able to save completely those who draw near to God through Him, since He always lives to intercede for them.

Through Him we have access to the Father.

Ephesians 2:18For through Him we both have access to the Father by one Spirit.

He told us to ask in His name.

John 14:13–14And I will do whatever you ask in My name, so that the Father may be glorified in the Son. If you ask Me for anything in My name, I will do it.

Luke records Mary’s own words.

Luke 1:48For He has looked with favor on the humble state of His servant. From now on all generations will call me blessed.

Calling her blessed is not the same as needing her to carry our prayers, and we may ask others to pray with us without waiting for them before we speak to the Father.

1 Timothy 2:5For there is one God, and there is one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus.

Also see

Romans 8:34Who is there to condemn us? For Christ Jesus, who died, and more than that was raised to life, is at the right hand of God—and He is interceding for us.

John 16:23–24In that day you will no longer ask Me anything. Truly, truly, I tell you, whatever you ask the Father in My name, He will give you. Until now you have not asked for anything in My name. Ask and you will receive, so that your joy may be complete.

Matthew 6:6But when you pray, go into your inner room, shut your door, and pray to your Father, who is unseen. And your Father, who sees what is done in secret, will reward you.

Hebrews 10:19–22Therefore, brothers, since we have confidence to enter the Most Holy Place by the blood of Jesus, by the new and living way opened for us through the curtain of His body, and since we have a great priest over the house of God, let us draw near with a sincere heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled to cleanse us from a guilty conscience and our bodies washed with pure water.

1 John 2:1My little children, I am writing these things to you so that you will not sin. But if anyone does sin, we have an advocate before the Father—Jesus Christ, the Righteous One.

Common self-statements

  • God will not listen if I come by myself.
  • I am not holy enough to speak to the Father.
  • My prayer counts only if a priest, a saint, or Mary delivers it.
  • Jesus is too far above me to bother with my request.
  • I should be afraid to use His name directly.
  • Asking someone else is safer than coming to God.

Common logical errors

  • All-or-nothing thinking: If I am not holy enough in myself, I cannot come to God at all.
  • Discounting the positive: Ignoring the mediator Scripture already gives: the man Christ Jesus.
  • Mind reading: Deciding God will not hear a prayer that comes in Jesus’ name.
  • Emotional reasoning: Feeling unworthy and treating that feeling as a closed door.

Challenging questions

  • Am I grateful for people who pray with me, or am I treating them as a required bridge?
  • Does feeling unworthy cancel what this verse says about drawing near through Jesus?

    Hebrews 7:25Therefore He is able to save completely those who draw near to God through Him, since He always lives to intercede for them.

  • What did Jesus say about asking in His name?

    John 14:13–14And I will do whatever you ask in My name, so that the Father may be glorified in the Son. If you ask Me for anything in My name, I will do it.

  • Who is at God’s right hand, interceding for us?

    Romans 8:34Who is there to condemn us? For Christ Jesus, who died, and more than that was raised to life, is at the right hand of God—and He is interceding for us.

  • How may we draw near to God?

    Hebrews 10:19–22Therefore, brothers, since we have confidence to enter the Most Holy Place by the blood of Jesus, by the new and living way opened for us through the curtain of His body, and since we have a great priest over the house of God, let us draw near with a sincere heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled to cleanse us from a guilty conscience and our bodies washed with pure water.

  • Can I thank God for Mary, and still come to the Father through Christ alone?

Truth summary

I do not need a priest, a saint, or Mary to make God hear me. There is one mediator, the man Christ Jesus. I may approach the throne of grace with confidence, and I may ask the Father in His name.
