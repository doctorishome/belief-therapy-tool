Biological/Physical False Belief #8 · Chronic Pain

“I can't be happy as long as I hurt.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

Pain narrows attention, and joy can seem like something that belongs to healthy people. We may stop doing what we enjoy and give up on laughter, connection, and meaning until the pain is gone.

What Scripture teaches

Paul wrote, “I have learned to be content regardless of my circumstances,” and described himself as “Sorrowful, yet always rejoicing” (2 Corinthians 6:10). The psalmist says, “When anxiety overwhelms me, Your consolation delights my soul” (Psalm 94:19). Joy is not the absence of pain. It is a gift that can exist beside pain, in small and real moments, and in the nearness of God.

Common self-statements

  • Happiness is impossible while I hurt.
  • I do not deserve to enjoy anything.
  • Joy would be fake.
  • Hurting people cannot laugh.
  • I will be happy when the pain stops.
  • Nothing feels good.

Common logical errors

  • All-or-nothing thinking: Believing I am either pain-free and happy or miserable.
  • Fortune telling: Predicting that I cannot enjoy anything.
  • Emotional reasoning: Feeling pain and concluding joy is impossible.
  • Labeling: Calling myself unable to enjoy life.

Challenging questions

  • When have I felt a moment of peace or joy despite pain?
  • What small pleasures are still possible?
  • What did Paul and the psalmists experience in suffering?
  • Whom do I enjoy being with?
  • What would I tell a friend who felt this way?
  • What gentle joy can I plan this week?

Truth summary

Joy and pain can exist together. I can look for small gifts each day, receive God’s consolation, and enjoy what I can, without waiting for the pain to end.

Pick one or two that fit me
  • Small joys: Each day, name one moment of joy or peace.
