Core False Belief #16 · The World & the Future

“Something terrible is going to happen.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

A sense of coming doom often follows a sudden shock, such as an unexpected death, diagnosis, or accident, which teaches the mind that disaster can strike without warning. It can also grow in homes where parents were constantly worried, or where something bad really did happen often. Worry can feel like protection, as if expecting the worst could prevent it.

What Scripture teaches

Jesus told His followers not to worry about tomorrow, because each day has enough trouble of its own. Paul urged believers to bring every anxious thought to God in prayer with thanksgiving, and promised that God’s peace would guard their hearts and minds. The psalmist describes a person who does not fear bad news because his heart is steady, trusting in the Lord. Worry cannot add a single hour to life. God does not ask us to pretend bad things never happen; He asks us to trust Him with tomorrow.

Therefore do not worry about tomorrow, for tomorrow will worry about itself. Today has enough trouble of its own (Matthew 6:34).

For God has not given us a spirit of fear, but of power, love, and self-control (2 Timothy 1:7).

Common self-statements

  • I just know something bad is coming.
  • If I stop worrying, it will happen.
  • Every phone call might be bad news.
  • Things are too good; something will go wrong.
  • I need to be prepared for the worst.
  • I can’t shake this feeling of dread.

Common logical errors

  • Fortune telling: Predicting disaster without evidence.
  • Catastrophizing: Jumping from a small worry to the worst possible outcome.
  • Emotional reasoning: Believing danger is coming because I feel dread.
  • Magical thinking: Believing my worry somehow prevents bad things.

Challenging questions

  • What evidence do I have that something terrible is about to happen?
  • How many times have I felt this dread and nothing happened?
  • Does my worrying actually prevent anything?
  • If something hard did happen, how might God help me through it?
  • What does Jesus say about tomorrow?
  • What can I do today instead of worrying about tomorrow?

Truth summary

Hard things can happen, and I cannot prevent every one. But this feeling of dread is not a prophecy, and worry does not protect me. God holds tomorrow. I can bring my fears to Him, live today, and trust Him to help me with whatever comes.

  • Fears and outcomes: Each day, write down any fear about something terrible happening. A week later, note whether it happened.
  • My name in Scripture: Write a personal reminder based on my verse and read it aloud. Keep this application separate from the Bible quotation. For example, Matthew 6:34 becomes: “James can write: “I don’t have to carry tomorrow today. God will be with me when it comes.””

6

My action step this week

Do one thing that tests the lie in real life.

Choose steps you can do safely. If this belief comes with hopelessness or thoughts of ending your life, call or text 988 (the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), or call 911 in an emergency. You do not have to carry this alone.

  • Test your predictions. Keep a list of the bad things you predict for two weeks. At the end, count how many actually happened.
  • Set a worry time. Give yourself 15 minutes a day to write worries and pray over them. Outside that time, gently postpone worry.
  • Pray it through. When dread rises, pray Philippians 4:6–7 aloud, naming each fear and thanking God for one thing.
  • Get help for constant dread. If dread is constant or comes with panic attacks, talk with a doctor or counselor.
