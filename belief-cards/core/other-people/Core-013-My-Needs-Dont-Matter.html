Core False Belief #13 · Other People

“My needs don’t matter.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

Many people learn this in families where their needs were ignored, mocked, or treated as selfish, or where they had to take care of a parent or siblings. Some were praised for always putting others first, so they learned that having needs was bad. As adults they may say yes to everything, neglect rest and health, and feel resentful or exhausted without knowing why.

What Scripture teaches

Scripture shows a God who cares about our needs. He opens His hand and satisfies the desires of every living thing, and He promises to supply all our needs in Christ. When Elijah was exhausted and despairing, God gave him food and sleep before anything else. Jesus said to love your neighbor as yourself, which assumes that caring for yourself is normal. Serving others is good, but God never asks you to pretend you have no needs. Your needs matter to Him.

And my God will supply all your needs according to His glorious riches in Christ Jesus (Philippians 4:19).

Common self-statements

  • Other people’s needs come first, always.
  • It’s selfish to want anything for myself.
  • I don’t want to be a bother.
  • I can rest when everything else is done.
  • If I say no, people will be upset.
  • My feelings aren’t important.

Common logical errors

  • All-or-nothing thinking: Believing I must either meet every need of others or be selfish.
  • Should statements: Telling myself I should never need anything.
  • Discounting the positive: Ignoring the times my needs were met and it was good.
  • Mind reading: Assuming others will be upset if I express a need.

Challenging questions

  • Where did I learn that my needs don’t matter?
  • What needs am I ignoring right now?
  • Is caring for myself really selfish?
  • How did God care for Elijah when he was exhausted?
  • What happens to me when I ignore my needs for too long?
  • What is one need I could honestly express this week?

Truth summary

I care about others, and that is good. But my needs matter too, to God and to the people who love me. God supplies my needs and cares about my body, rest, and feelings. I can take care of myself and still serve others.

6

My action step this week

Do one thing that tests the lie in real life.

Choose steps you can do safely. If this belief comes with hopelessness or thoughts of ending your life, call or text 988 (the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), or call 911 in an emergency. You do not have to carry this alone.

  • Name your needs. Write a list of what you need: rest, food, friendship, quiet, help. Circle one to address this week.
  • Say one honest no. Decline one request this week that you would normally accept out of guilt. Notice what happens.
  • Schedule rest. Put one hour of rest or enjoyment on your calendar and keep it like an appointment.
  • Express a need. Tell one trusted person about a need you have, and let them help.
