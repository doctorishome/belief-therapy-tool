Core False Belief #11 · Other People

“Nobody loves me.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

This belief can grow from a childhood without warmth or affection, the loss of a spouse or parent, a painful breakup, or long seasons of loneliness. Depression can also color everything gray, so that even real love is hard to feel. When people feel unloved, they may withdraw, which leads to more loneliness and seems to prove the belief true.

What Scripture teaches

The Bible declares that God has loved His people with an everlasting love. John marvels at the kind of love the Father has given, that we should be called children of God. Jesus said that as the Father loved Him, so He loves us. Even if mountains are shaken, God’s loving devotion will not depart. Human love may be missing or imperfect right now, but you are loved by God fully and personally. That love can also open your eyes to the people who do care for you.

Behold what manner of love the Father has given to us, that we should be called children of God. And that is what we are! The reason the world does not know us is that it did not know Him (1 John 3:1).

Also see

The LORD appeared to us in the past, saying: ‘I have loved you with an everlasting love; therefore I have drawn you with loving devotion.’ (Jeremiah 31:3).

As the Father has loved Me, so have I loved you. Remain in My love (John 15:9).

Though the mountains may be removed and the hills may be shaken, My loving devotion will not depart from you, and My covenant of peace will not be broken, says the LORD, who has compassion on you (Isaiah 54:10).

As a father has compassion on his children, so the LORD has compassion on those who fear Him (Psalm 103:13).

Common self-statements

  • No one cares about me.
  • If I died, no one would miss me.
  • Everyone else is loved but me.
  • People are nice to me only out of obligation.
  • I’m completely alone.
  • Even God doesn’t love me.

Common logical errors

  • Overgeneralization: Using “nobody” and “never” when some people do care.
  • Emotional reasoning: Believing I am unloved because I feel unloved.
  • Discounting the positive: Explaining away kindness as obligation.
  • Mental filter: Focusing on who doesn’t love me and overlooking who does.

Challenging questions

  • Is it true that nobody loves me, or that I don’t feel loved right now?
  • Who has shown me care, even in small ways?
  • Am I withdrawing in ways that make love harder to find?
  • Could sadness or depression be affecting how I see things?
  • What does God say about His love for me?
  • What is one small step toward connection?

Truth summary

I feel unloved right now, and that is painful. But feelings are not the full truth. God has loved me with an everlasting love and calls me His child. I can look for the ways I am cared for and take small steps toward people.

6

My action step this week

Do one thing that tests the lie in real life.

Choose steps you can do safely. If this belief comes with hopelessness or thoughts of ending your life, call or text 988 (the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), or call 911 in an emergency. You do not have to carry this alone.

  • Look for evidence. Write the names of people who have shown you any kindness in the last year. Ask yourself what each act says.
  • Reach out once. Send one message or make one call to someone you care about this week.
  • Read God’s love letter. Read 1 John 3:1 and Jeremiah 31:3 each morning and say, “This is true of me.”
  • Get support for heavy sadness. If feeling unloved comes with deep sadness that won’t lift, talk with a doctor, pastor, or counselor.
