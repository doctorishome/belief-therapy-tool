Spiritual/Religious False Belief #33 · Truth

“If a teaching makes me feel good, it must be from God.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

Popular preaching that focuses mainly on comfort, success, and self-improvement. Past hurt from harsh teaching, which makes any challenge feel unsafe. Wanting God’s Word to always encourage and never correct. Social media and books that sound spiritual but are mostly positive thinking. Confusing emotional uplift with spiritual truth.

What Scripture teaches

God’s Word does bring comfort, hope, and joy. But Scripture also warns that people will gather teachers who say what their itching ears want to hear. God’s Word is living and active and sometimes cuts deeply, correcting us for our good. A friend’s honest wounds can be more faithful than an enemy’s kisses. The test of a teaching is whether it is true to Scripture, not whether it makes us feel good.

For the time will come when men will not tolerate sound doctrine, but with itching ears they will gather around themselves teachers to suit their own desires. So they will turn their ears away from the truth and turn aside to myths (2 Timothy 4:3–4).

Also see

For the word of God is living and active. Sharper than any double-edged sword, it pierces even to dividing soul and spirit, joints and marrow. It judges the thoughts and intentions of the heart (Hebrews 4:12).

All Scripture is God-breathed and is useful for instruction, for conviction, for correction, and for training in righteousness, so that the man of God may be complete, fully equipped for every good work (2 Timothy 3:16–17).

Am I now seeking the approval of men, or of God? Or am I striving to please men? If I were still trying to please men, I would not be a servant of Christ (Galatians 1:10).

The wounds of a friend are faithful, but the kisses of an enemy are deceitful (Proverbs 27:6).

Common self-statements

  • That sermon felt great, so it must be true.
  • God would never say anything that makes me uncomfortable.
  • If a teaching challenges me, it must be wrong.
  • Real Christianity always makes you feel good.
  • I only listen to teachers who encourage me.
  • Correction is from the enemy.

Common logical errors

  • Emotional reasoning: Judging a teaching by how it feels rather than by Scripture.
  • Selective attention: Accepting comforting verses and avoiding challenging ones.
  • All-or-nothing thinking: Believing truth must be either all comfort or all harshness.
  • Confirmation bias: Seeking out only teachers who confirm what you want.

Challenging questions

  • What does 2 Timothy 4:3–4 say about teachers who suit our desires?
  • Does God’s Word ever correct or challenge me?
  • Is this teaching true to all of Scripture, or just to the parts I like?
  • Have I ever needed a hard truth that didn’t feel good at first?
  • Am I avoiding a message because it’s wrong, or because it’s uncomfortable?
  • What does Hebrews 4:12 say God’s Word does?
  • Can truth be loving and still be hard to hear?

Truth summary

A teaching is not from God just because it makes me feel good. God’s Word brings comfort, but it also corrects and challenges me for my good. I can test every teaching by Scripture, receive both encouragement and correction, and trust that God’s truth is always loving, even when it is hard.
