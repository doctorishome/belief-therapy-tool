Spiritual/Religious False Belief #22 · The Soul: Emotions, Body & Mind

“Depression and anxiety are always caused by sin.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

Teaching that every emotional struggle comes from unconfessed sin or lack of faith. Well-meaning friends who asked, “What are you doing wrong?” when you were hurting. Not knowing that the body, the brain, life events, and loss all affect mood. Shame that made you hide your struggles. Times when a sin did make things worse, so you assumed it always must be the cause.

What Scripture teaches

Sin can contribute to emotional pain, but Scripture does not teach that it is always the cause. When the disciples asked whose sin made a man blind, Jesus said neither. Elijah, a faithful prophet, became so exhausted and discouraged that he asked to die, and God responded with rest, food, and gentle care. Paul despaired even of life. Depression and anxiety can come from many sources: body, brain, loss, stress, trauma, thinking patterns, and spiritual struggles. God meets you in all of them.

And His disciples asked Him, ‘Rabbi, who sinned, this man or his parents, that he was born blind?’ Jesus answered, ‘Neither this man nor his parents sinned, but this happened so that the works of God would be displayed in him.’ (John 9:2–3).

While he himself traveled on a day’s journey into the wilderness. He sat down under a broom tree and prayed that he might die. ‘I have had enough, LORD,’ he said. ‘Take my life, for I am no better than my fathers.’ (1 Kings 19:4).

We do not want you to be unaware, brothers, of the hardships we encountered in the province of Asia. We were under a burden far beyond our ability to endure, so that we despaired even of life. Indeed, we felt we were under the sentence of death, in order that we would not trust in ourselves, but in God, who raises the dead (2 Corinthians 1:8–9).

The LORD is near to the brokenhearted; He saves the contrite in spirit (Psalm 34:18).

He heals the brokenhearted and binds up their wounds (Psalm 147:3).

Common self-statements

  • If I weren’t sinning, I wouldn’t be depressed.
  • My anxiety proves I don’t trust God.
  • I must have done something to deserve this.
  • God is punishing me with these feelings.
  • If I confess enough, this will go away.
  • Christians who are depressed aren’t right with God.

Common logical errors

  • Overgeneralization: Turning “sin can cause distress” into “all distress is caused by sin.”
  • Personalization: Blaming yourself for something with many causes.
  • Jumping to conclusions: Deciding the cause without considering the body, stress, or loss.
  • Mental filter: Ignoring Bible heroes who struggled deeply without sinning as the cause.
  • Shame-based thinking: Treating a painful condition as proof of moral failure.

Challenging questions

  • What did Jesus say when the disciples blamed sin for the man’s blindness?
  • Was Elijah’s despair caused by sin, or by exhaustion and fear?
  • Did God scold Elijah, or did He feed him and let him sleep?
  • What other causes could be affecting my mood: sleep, health, stress, loss?
  • Have I confessed and repented and still feel this way? What might that tell me?
  • Would I say this to a friend who was depressed?
  • Who first taught me that emotional pain always means sin?

Truth summary

Depression and anxiety are not always caused by sin. They can come from my body, my brain, loss, stress, my thinking, or many other things. Faithful people in the Bible felt deep despair. God is near to the brokenhearted, and He meets me with care, not condemnation. I can seek His help and every good help He provides.
