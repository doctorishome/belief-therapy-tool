Spiritual/Religious False Belief #23 · The Soul: Emotions, Body & Mind

“Counseling or medication shows a lack of faith.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

Hearing that Christians should “just pray more” instead of getting help. Distrust of psychology because some counselors dismissed faith. Fear of what others at church would think. Pride or embarrassment about needing help. Believing that if God wanted to heal you, He would do it without any human means.

What Scripture teaches

God often works through people and practical means. Scripture praises wise counsel: plans succeed with many advisers. Luke was called “the beloved physician.” Paul told Timothy to take something for his stomach, and Isaiah had figs applied to King Hezekiah’s boil after God promised healing. Jesus said the sick need a doctor. Seeking good counsel and medical care can be an act of faith and stewardship, trusting God to work through the help He provides.

Plans fail for lack of counsel, but with many advisers they succeed (Proverbs 15:22).

Also see

For lack of guidance, a nation falls, but with many counselors comes deliverance (Proverbs 11:14).

On hearing this, Jesus said, ‘It is not the healthy who need a doctor, but the sick.’ (Matthew 9:12).

Stop drinking only water and use a little wine instead, because of your stomach and your frequent ailments (1 Timothy 5:23).

Luke, the beloved physician, and Demas send you greetings (Colossians 4:14).

Common self-statements

  • If I had more faith, I wouldn’t need help.
  • Taking medicine means I don’t trust God.
  • Counseling is for people who aren’t right with God.
  • I should be able to pray this away.
  • People at church will think less of me.
  • Getting help means I’ve failed spiritually.

Common logical errors

  • All-or-nothing thinking: Believing you must choose between trusting God and getting help.
  • Should statements: “I should be able to handle this with prayer alone.”
  • Mind reading: Assuming everyone at church will judge you.
  • Double standard: Accepting help for a broken arm but not for a hurting mind.
  • Discounting Scripture: Ignoring passages where God works through advisers and physicians.

Challenging questions

  • Would I refuse a cast for a broken arm to prove my faith?
  • Why does Scripture praise many advisers if counsel shows weak faith?
  • Did Paul tell Timothy to stop trusting God when he advised a remedy?
  • Could God be answering my prayers through a counselor or doctor?
  • Is my reluctance about faith, or about pride or fear?
  • Can I pray and get help at the same time?
  • What would I tell a friend in my situation?

Truth summary

Getting counseling or medication does not mean I lack faith. God often works through wise counsel and good medical care. I can pray and seek help at the same time, trusting God to use the people and tools He provides. Caring for my mind and body is good stewardship.
