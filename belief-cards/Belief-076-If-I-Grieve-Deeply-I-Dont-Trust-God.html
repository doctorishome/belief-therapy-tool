Spiritual/Religious False Belief #76 · Death & Grief

“If I grieve deeply, I don’t trust God.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

Hearing “They’re in a better place, so don’t be sad.” A church culture that expects quick recovery from loss. Feeling pressure to look strong for others. Confusing hope with the absence of sadness. Guilt when tears keep coming months or years later.

What Scripture teaches

Grief is the price of love, and Scripture does not forbid it. Jesus knew He was about to raise Lazarus, and still He wept. Paul did not tell believers not to grieve, but not to grieve like those without hope. Jesus said those who mourn are blessed, for they will be comforted. God is near to the brokenhearted and keeps track of every tear. Deep grief and deep trust can live in the same heart.

Brothers, we do not want you to be uninformed about those who sleep in death, so that you will not grieve like the rest, who are without hope. For since we believe that Jesus died and rose again, we also believe that God will bring with Jesus those who have fallen asleep in Him (1 Thessalonians 4:13–14).

Also see

When Jesus saw her weeping, and the Jews who had come with her also weeping, He was deeply moved in spirit and troubled. ‘Where have you put him?’ He asked. ‘Come and see, Lord,’ they answered. Jesus wept (John 11:33–35).

Blessed are those who mourn, for they will be comforted (Matthew 5:4).

You have taken account of my wanderings. Put my tears in Your bottle— are they not in Your book? (Psalm 56:8).

Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of compassion and the God of all comfort, who comforts us in all our troubles, so that we can comfort those in any trouble with the comfort we ourselves have received from God (2 Corinthians 1:3–4).

Common self-statements

  • If I really trusted God, I wouldn’t be this sad.
  • I should be over this by now.
  • My tears show weak faith.
  • I have to be strong for everyone.
  • Christians shouldn’t grieve this much.
  • I’m letting God down by hurting so badly.

Common logical errors

  • All-or-nothing thinking: Believing you must either trust God or grieve, but not both.
  • Should statements: “I should be over this by now.”
  • Comparison: Measuring your grief against how others seem to cope.
  • Misreading Scripture: Hearing “do not grieve like the rest” as “do not grieve at all.”

Challenging questions

  • Did Jesus weep, even knowing He would raise Lazarus?
  • What did Paul actually say about grief in 1 Thessalonians 4:13?
  • What does Jesus promise to those who mourn?
  • Does God keep track of my tears or scold me for them?
  • Can I grieve and hope at the same time?
  • Who told me grief means weak faith?
  • What kind of comfort do I need right now?

Truth summary

Grieving deeply does not mean I don’t trust God. Jesus wept, and God keeps track of my tears. I can grieve with hope, trusting that God will bring with Jesus those who have fallen asleep in Him. Blessed are those who mourn, for they will be comforted.
