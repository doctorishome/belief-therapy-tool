Spiritual/Religious False Belief #42 · Salvation

“I can never really know whether I am saved.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

Teaching that stressed fear and uncertainty about salvation. Worry that you did not pray the right prayer or did not mean it enough. Ongoing struggles with sin that make you doubt. An anxious personality that seeks certainty and reassurance. Comparing your faith with others who seem more confident.

What Scripture teaches

God wants you to know that you have eternal life. John wrote his letter so that those who believe in the Son may know they have eternal life. Jesus said that whoever hears His word and believes has eternal life and has crossed over from death to life. Assurance rests on God’s promises and Christ’s finished work, not on the strength of your feelings. The Spirit Himself testifies with your spirit that you are God’s child.

I have written these things to you who believe in the name of the Son of God, so that you may know that you have eternal life (1 John 5:13).

Also see

Truly, truly, I tell you, whoever hears My word and believes Him who sent Me has eternal life and will not come under judgment. Indeed, he has crossed over from death to life (John 5:24).

That if you confess with your mouth, ‘Jesus is Lord,’ and believe in your heart that God raised Him from the dead, you will be saved. For with your heart you believe and are justified, and with your mouth you confess and are saved (Romans 10:9–10).

I give them eternal life, and they will never perish. No one can snatch them out of My hand. My Father who has given them to Me is greater than all. No one can snatch them out of My Father’s hand (John 10:28–29).

The Spirit Himself testifies with our spirit that we are God’s children (Romans 8:16).

Common self-statements

  • I can never be sure I’m saved.
  • Maybe I didn’t mean it enough.
  • If I really were saved, I wouldn’t doubt.
  • I have to keep checking whether I’m saved.
  • Only super-spiritual people can be sure.
  • It would be prideful to say I know I’m saved.

Common logical errors

  • Emotional reasoning: Concluding “I don’t feel sure, so I must not be saved.”
  • Checking and reassurance-seeking: Repeatedly testing your salvation, which only increases doubt.
  • Perfectionism: Believing your faith must be perfect to be real.
  • Discounting God’s promises: Trusting your feelings more than God’s Word.

Challenging questions

  • Why did John write 1 John, according to 5:13?
  • What does Jesus say about whoever hears and believes?
  • Is my assurance based on my feelings or on God’s promises?
  • Who holds me: do I hold on to God, or does He hold me?
  • Is it prideful to believe what God says?
  • Does checking over and over make me more or less sure?
  • Have I put my trust in Jesus? Then what does God say about me?

Truth summary

I can know that I am saved. God wants me to know I have eternal life. If I believe in Jesus, I have crossed over from death to life, and no one can snatch me out of His hand. My assurance rests on God’s promises and Christ’s work, not on my feelings.

  • Promise log: When doubt comes, write one promise of God about salvation instead of re-checking my feelings.

My action step this week

Do one thing that tests the lie in real life.

  • Write down the date and story of when you trusted Christ, or write today’s date and pray a simple prayer of trust now.
  • When doubt comes, read John 5:24 once and then go on with your day without re-checking.
  • Read 1 John straight through, and underline every time John says “we know.”
  • Talk with a pastor about assurance if checking has become a heavy habit.
