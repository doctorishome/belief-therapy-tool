Psychological/Cognitive False Belief #76 · Substance Abuse

“A little doesn’t count. I can keep it small and still be in control.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

“A little doesn’t count” is how a lot of people stay out of the conversation. If it is small, it is not a problem, and you are still in control. Sometimes the amount really is small. The lie is the rule that small means harmless, and that you can keep it small forever by deciding to. You do not need a dramatic story to be allowed to get help. You also do not need to call yourself a terrible person because the amount was not huge. Honesty about the little is an act of care, not a confession that you are worthless. If you want help, call the SAMHSA National Helpline at 1-800-662-4357. Do not try to stop or withdraw on your own if that could make you sick. A doctor can help you do it safely, and a counselor can walk with you. Needing that help is not a failure.

What Scripture teaches

Paul says that not everything is beneficial, and “I will not be mastered by anything.” He is not sorting people into the ruined and the fine. He is telling the truth that a permitted thing can still take the lead. You do not know what tomorrow brings, so “I will keep it small” is a hope, not a guarantee. You can ask God for help before a little becomes the master. Jesus sets people free. Your help comes from the Lord. None of this requires you to exaggerate the problem or to hide it.

‘Everything is permissible for me,’ but not everything is beneficial. ‘Everything is permissible for me,’ but I will not be mastered by anything (1 Corinthians 6:12).

Common self-statements

  • It is only a little. People are overreacting.
  • I can keep it at this level forever.
  • If I am not using as much as someone else, I do not need help.
  • Admitting the little makes me a hypocrite.
  • I am still in charge because I chose the amount.
  • God does not care about something this small.

Common logical errors

  • Minimization: Shrinking the pattern so you do not have to look at it.
  • Comparison: Using someone else’s larger use as proof that yours does not count.
  • All-or-nothing thinking: If it is not the worst case, it is nothing.
  • Control fallacy: Believing that choosing the amount means the amount cannot start choosing you.

Challenging questions

  • What do I mean by a little, and has that meaning already changed?
  • What am I unwilling to give up, even in a small amount?
  • Would I want a friend to get help at this stage, or only after a crisis?
  • Am I more interested in keeping it small or in telling the truth?
  • What would help look like before things grow?
  • What does “not mastered by anything” invite, without a speech of shame?

Truth summary

A little can still matter. I can be honest about it, and I can get help before I have to prove I lost control.

My action step this week

Do one thing that tests the lie in real life.

Choose steps you can do safely. If this belief comes with hopelessness or thoughts of ending your life, call or text 988 (the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), or call 911 in an emergency. For help with substance use, call 1-800-662-4357, and talk with a doctor before you stop if withdrawal could make you sick.

  • Write the actual amount for the last seven days, plainly. No comparing it with someone worse, and no name-calling.
  • Show that note to a doctor, a counselor, or tell it to 1-800-662-4357.
  • Do not “prove it is small” by stopping suddenly if withdrawal could make you sick. Ask what is safe.
  • Pray 1 Corinthians 6:12 as a request not to be mastered, not as a verdict.
