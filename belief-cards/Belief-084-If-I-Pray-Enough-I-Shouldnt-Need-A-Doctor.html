Spiritual/Religious False Belief #84 · Prayer

“If I pray enough, I shouldn’t need a doctor or counselor.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

Teaching that seeking medical or psychological help shows weak faith. Stories of healing through prayer alone. Fear of what others at church would think. Distrust of doctors or counselors. Pride or embarrassment about needing help.

What Scripture teaches

Prayer and practical help go together in Scripture. James tells the sick to call the elders to pray over them and anoint them with oil, which in that time was also used as medicine. The Good Samaritan bandaged wounds with oil and wine. Luke was a beloved physician. Jesus said the sick need a doctor. Praying and seeking care are not opposites; God often answers prayer through doctors and counselors.

Is any one of you sick? He should call the elders of the church to pray over him and anoint him with oil in the name of the Lord. And the prayer offered in faith will restore the one who is sick. The Lord will raise him up. If he has sinned, he will be forgiven (James 5:14–15).

Also see

He went to him and bandaged his wounds, pouring on oil and wine. Then he put him on his own animal, brought him to an inn, and took care of him (Luke 10:34).

On hearing this, Jesus said, ‘It is not the healthy who need a doctor, but the sick.’ (Matthew 9:12).

Luke, the beloved physician, and Demas send you greetings (Colossians 4:14).

Plans fail for lack of counsel, but with many advisers they succeed (Proverbs 15:22).

Common self-statements

  • If I pray enough, I won’t need help.
  • Seeing a doctor means I don’t trust God.
  • Counseling is for people with weak faith.
  • God will be disappointed if I get treatment.
  • My prayers should be enough.
  • Taking medicine is giving up on God.

Common logical errors

  • All-or-nothing thinking: Believing you must choose between prayer and help.
  • Should statements: “I should be able to pray this away.”
  • Discounting Scripture: Overlooking examples of prayer and care together.
  • Double standard: Accepting help for some problems but not others.

Challenging questions

  • What did James tell the sick to do besides pray?
  • How did the Good Samaritan care for the wounded man?
  • What did Jesus say about who needs a doctor?
  • Could God answer my prayers through a doctor or counselor?
  • Would I tell a friend with a broken bone to only pray?
  • Is my hesitation about faith, or about fear or pride?
  • What help might God be offering me right now?

Truth summary

Praying and getting help are not opposites. God often answers prayer through doctors, counselors, and medicine. I can pray with faith and also seek wise care. Seeking help is good stewardship of the life God gave me, not a lack of faith.
