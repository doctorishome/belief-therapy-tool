Spiritual/Religious False Belief #9 · Christ

“Jesus doesn’t understand what I’m going through.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

A lonely illness, a grief no one else shares, a temptation you are ashamed to name, or a picture of Jesus as only distant and divine can make Him seem unable to understand. If He never married, never lost this job, or never lived in your city, the mind decides He cannot know this pain. Scripture says something stronger than “He has a theory about suffering.” He took on flesh, was tempted, sorrowed, and wept.

What Scripture teaches

The Son of God did not skip the human part.

Hebrews 4:15For we do not have a high priest who is unable to sympathize with our weaknesses, but we have one who was tempted in every way that we are, yet was without sin.

He suffered when He was tempted, so He is able to help.

Hebrews 2:17–18For this reason He had to be made like His brothers in every way, so that He might become a merciful and faithful high priest in service to God, in order to make atonement for the sins of the people. Because He Himself suffered when He was tempted, He is able to help those who are being tempted.

He was a man of sorrows, acquainted with grief.

Isaiah 53:3He was despised and rejected by men, a man of sorrows, acquainted with grief. Like one from whom men hide their faces, He was despised, and we esteemed Him not.

At a friend’s grave, Jesus wept.

John 11:35Jesus wept.

Because He understands, we may draw near with confidence.

Hebrews 4:14–16Therefore, since we have a great high priest who has passed through the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, let us hold firmly to what we profess. For we do not have a high priest who is unable to sympathize with our weaknesses, but we have one who was tempted in every way that we are, yet was without sin. Let us then approach the throne of grace with confidence, so that we may receive mercy and find grace to help us in our time of need.

He does not have to have held your exact job to know weakness, temptation, grief, and sorrow. Because He suffered when He was tempted, He is able to help. You may approach the throne of grace with confidence.

Also see

Isaiah 53:4Surely He took on our infirmities and carried our sorrows; yet we considered Him stricken by God, struck down and afflicted.

Hebrews 5:7–8During the days of Jesus’ earthly life, He offered up prayers and petitions with loud cries and tears to the One who could save Him from death, and He was heard because of His reverence. Although He was a Son, He learned obedience from what He suffered.

Matthew 8:17This was to fulfill what was spoken through the prophet Isaiah: ‘He took on our infirmities and carried our diseases.’

Psalm 22:24For He has not despised or detested the torment of the afflicted. He has not hidden His face from him, but has attended to his cry for help.

John 1:14The Word became flesh and made His dwelling among us. We have seen His glory, the glory of the one and only Son from the Father, full of grace and truth.

Common self-statements

  • Jesus cannot know what this feels like.
  • He was too holy to understand my temptation.
  • My pain is a modern problem He never faced.
  • If He understood, He would have stopped it.
  • I need someone who has lived my exact story.
  • I should not bother Him with something this human.

Common logical errors

  • All-or-nothing thinking: If Jesus did not live my exact details, He cannot understand me at all.
  • Mind reading: Deciding what the Son of God is unable to grasp.
  • Discounting the positive: Ignoring that He was tempted, suffered, sorrowed, and wept.
  • Emotional reasoning: Feeling alone and concluding that Christ is a stranger to this pain.

Challenging questions

  • What part of this pain have I decided is off-limits to Jesus?
  • What did Jesus say His soul felt in Gethsemane?

    Matthew 26:38Then He said to them, ‘My soul is consumed with sorrow to the point of death. Stay here and keep watch with Me.’

  • Why did the Son share flesh and blood?

    Hebrews 2:14Now since the children have flesh and blood, He too shared in their humanity, so that by His death He might destroy him who holds the power of death, that is, the devil.

  • Whose affliction did the Lord share?

    Isaiah 63:9In all their distress, He too was afflicted, and the Angel of His Presence saved them. In His love and compassion He redeemed them; He lifted them up and carried them all the days of old.

  • What did Jesus say from the cross about His thirst?

    John 19:28After this, knowing that everything had now been accomplished, and to fulfill the Scripture, Jesus said, ‘I am thirsty.’

  • What would I say to Him today if I believed He understands and is able to help?

Truth summary

Jesus does understand what I am going through. He was tempted as we are, He was a man of sorrows, and He wept. Because He suffered, He is able to help, and I may come to Him with confidence.
