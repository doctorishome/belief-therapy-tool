Spiritual/Religious False Belief #99 · Marriage, Sex & Family

“If my children turn away from God, I failed as a parent.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

Reading Proverbs 22:6 as a guarantee. Comments from others that blame parents. Regret over mistakes you made while raising your children. Grief over a child who has walked away from faith. Comparing your family with others whose children seem faithful.

What Scripture teaches

Parents matter, and we are called to raise children in the Lord. But each person makes their own choices. Through Isaiah, God Himself said, “I have raised children and brought them up, but they have rebelled against Me.” God is the perfect Parent, and His children still rebelled. In Jesus’ story, the prodigal son left a loving father. Proverbs describes wisdom, not a guarantee. God is still at work in your children, and He welcomes prodigals home.

Listen, O heavens, and give ear, O earth, for the LORD has spoken: ‘I have raised children and brought them up, but they have rebelled against Me.’ (Isaiah 1:2).

Also see

The soul who sins is the one who will die. A son will not bear the iniquity of his father, and a father will not bear the iniquity of his son. The righteousness of the righteous man will fall upon him, and the wickedness of the wicked man will fall upon him (Ezekiel 18:20).

So he got up and went to his father. But while he was still in the distance, his father saw him and was filled with compassion. He ran to his son, embraced him, and kissed him (Luke 15:20).

I planted the seed and Apollos watered it, but God made it grow. So neither he who plants nor he who waters is anything, but only God, who makes things grow (1 Corinthians 3:6–7).

Because of the loving devotion of the LORD we are not consumed, for His mercies never fail. They are new every morning; great is Your faithfulness! (Lamentations 3:22–23).

Common self-statements

  • It’s my fault my child left the faith.
  • If I had been a better parent, they’d still believe.
  • Proverbs 22:6 proves I failed.
  • Everyone at church blames me.
  • God is disappointed in my parenting.
  • There’s no hope for my child.

Common logical errors

  • Personalization: Taking full responsibility for another person’s choices.
  • Reading proverbs as promises: Treating a general principle as a guarantee.
  • Hindsight bias: Judging your past parenting by what you know now.
  • Fortune telling: Concluding your child will never return.

Challenging questions

  • Did God’s own children rebel against Him?
  • Was the prodigal’s father a bad parent?
  • Is Proverbs 22:6 a promise or a general principle?
  • Does my child have their own choices before God?
  • What can I still do: pray, love, and stay connected?
  • Is God finished working in my child’s life?
  • What mistakes can I bring to God and then release?

Truth summary

If my children turn away from God, it does not automatically mean I failed. Even God’s children rebelled against Him. My children make their own choices. I can confess my mistakes, keep loving and praying for them, and trust that God is still at work. He welcomes prodigals home.
