Spiritual/Religious False Belief #4 · God

“God is too distant or too busy to care about me.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

A parent who was absent, emotionally distant, or too busy to notice you. Growing up feeling that your needs were a bother. Prayers that seemed to go unanswered during a painful time. Teaching that pictured God as far off and unapproachable. Seasons of loneliness or depression, when God felt silent.

What Scripture teaches

God is not too busy for you. He knows when a sparrow falls, and He has numbered the hairs on your head. He is close to the brokenhearted, and He invites you to bring every worry to Him because He cares for you. There is nowhere you can go that is outside His presence. Not feeling close to God does not mean He has moved away.

Cast all your anxiety on him because he cares for you (1 Peter 5:7).

Also see

The LORD is close to the brokenhearted and saves those who are crushed in spirit (Psalm 34:18).

Are not two sparrows sold for a penny? Yet not one of them will fall to the ground outside your Father’s care. And even the very hairs of your head are all numbered. So don’t be afraid; you are worth more than many sparrows (Matthew 10:29–31).

Where can I go from your Spirit? Where can I flee from your presence? (Psalm 139:7).

He will not let your foot slip—he who watches over you will not slumber; indeed, he who watches over Israel will neither slumber nor sleep (Psalm 121:3–4).

Never will I leave you; never will I forsake you (Hebrews 13:5).

Common self-statements

  • God has bigger things to worry about than me.
  • My problems are too small for God to care about.
  • God is far away.
  • I pray, but nobody is listening.
  • God takes care of other people, but not me.
  • I’m on my own.

Common logical errors

  • Emotional reasoning: Concluding “I don’t feel God near me, so He must be far away.”
  • Projection: Picturing God as distant and busy like a parent who had no time for you.
  • Minimization: Deciding your needs are too small to matter to God.
  • Mental filter: Remembering the prayers that seemed unanswered and forgetting the times God provided.
  • Jumping to conclusions: Treating God’s timing or silence as proof that He does not care.
  • Overgeneralization: Taking one lonely season as proof that God has always been, and will always be, distant.

Challenging questions

  • What is the actual evidence that God does not care about me?
  • Am I judging God’s nearness by my feelings instead of by His promises?
  • Who in my life was too busy for me? Am I picturing God like that person?
  • If God keeps track of sparrows and the hairs on my head, is any worry of mine too small for Him?
  • Have I forgotten times when God did provide, comfort, or protect me?
  • Does Scripture say God is close to the brokenhearted, or far from them?

Truth summary

God is not too distant or too busy to care about me. He watches over me without sleeping, He knows every detail of my life, and He is close to me when I am brokenhearted. He invites me to bring all my worries to Him because He cares for me. Even when I cannot feel Him, He has promised never to leave me.

  • Set reminders to silently repeat my truth. Check one or more: ☐ A buzz: my watch or phone vibrates every ______ minutes
    ☐ An everyday event: every time I ______________________________
    ☐ A visual reminder: something I will see often, such as a sticky note on my mirror, my truth as the background picture on my phone, or a note on my car dashboard.
Pick one or two that fit me
  • Evidence log: Each day, write down one way God cared for me or provided for me today.
  • My name in Scripture: Rewrite my verse with my name in it and read it aloud each morning. For example, 1 Peter 5:7 becomes: “Sarah, cast all your anxiety on him because he cares for you.”
  • Gratitude journal: Each night, write three things I thank God for.
  • Truth recording: Record my own voice reading my Truth Card and verse, and listen in the car or on a walk.
  • Reading: The “God, Present Everywhere” topic in Pearls.
  • Listening: The Overcoming Depression audio download.

My action step this week

Do one thing that tests the lie in real life.

  • Bring one “small” worry to God in prayer each day for a week, and write down what happens.
  • Make a “remember” list of five times in your life when God provided, protected, or comforted you.
  • Reach out to one safe person and share a need, instead of assuming you are a bother.
  • Live “as if” for one day: act the way a person who believes God is near and cares would act.
