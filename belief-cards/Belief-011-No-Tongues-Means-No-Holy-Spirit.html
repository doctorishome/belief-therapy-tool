Spiritual/Religious False Belief #11 · Holy Spirit

“If I don’t speak in tongues, I don’t have the Holy Spirit.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

A church where speaking in tongues was treated as the main proof of being a real believer. Feeling left out or judged when others prayed in tongues and you did not. Pressure to produce an experience you never had. Comparing your quiet faith with other people’s more expressive worship. Christians differ on the place of tongues today, but Scripture is clear on the main question: every believer has the Spirit.

What Scripture teaches

Every person who belongs to Christ has the Holy Spirit. Believers are sealed with the Spirit when they hear and believe the gospel. Paul asks, “Do all speak in tongues?” and the expected answer is no. The Spirit gives different gifts to different people, as He chooses. The clearest evidence of His presence is not one gift, but His fruit: love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control.

And in Him, having heard and believed the word of truth—the gospel of your salvation—you were sealed with the promised Holy Spirit, who is the pledge of our inheritance until the redemption of those who are God’s possession, to the praise of His glory (Ephesians 1:13–14).

Also see

You, however, are controlled not by the flesh, but by the Spirit, if the Spirit of God lives in you. And if anyone does not have the Spirit of Christ, he does not belong to Christ (Romans 8:9).

For in one Spirit we were all baptized into one body, whether Jews or Greeks, slave or free, and we were all given one Spirit to drink (1 Corinthians 12:13).

Are all apostles? Are all prophets? Are all teachers? Do all work miracles? Do all have gifts of healing? Do all speak in tongues? Do all interpret? But eagerly desire the greater gifts. And now I will show you the most excellent way (1 Corinthians 12:29–31).

But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control. Against such things there is no law (Galatians 5:22–23).

Common self-statements

  • I must not really be saved.
  • Something is wrong with me spiritually.
  • God gave the Spirit to everyone but me.
  • I’m a second-class Christian.
  • If I had more faith, I would speak in tongues.
  • Others are closer to God than I am.

Common logical errors

  • Overgeneralization: Turning one gift that some receive into a test everyone must pass.
  • All-or-nothing thinking: Believing you either have this gift or have no Spirit at all.
  • Comparison: Measuring your walk with God by someone else’s experience.
  • Disqualifying the positive: Ignoring the fruit of the Spirit already growing in your life.
  • Emotional reasoning: Concluding “I feel left out, so God must have left me out.”

Challenging questions

  • What does Romans 8:9 say about everyone who belongs to Christ?
  • When Paul asks, “Do all speak in tongues?” what answer does he expect?
  • Who decides which gifts each believer receives?
  • What fruit of the Spirit can I see growing in my life?
  • Is this test coming from Scripture or from one group’s experience?
  • Did I believe in Christ? Then what does Ephesians 1:13 say happened?
  • Can I respect believers who speak in tongues without doubting my own salvation?

Truth summary

If I belong to Christ, I have the Holy Spirit. I was sealed with the Spirit when I believed the gospel. The Spirit gives different gifts to different people as He chooses, and no single gift is the test of His presence. I can look for His fruit in my life and use the gifts He has given me.
