Spiritual/Religious False Belief #77 · Death & Grief

“God took my loved one because He needed them more than I did.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

Comforting sayings at funerals: “God needed another angel” or “God needed them more.” Searching for a reason behind a painful loss. Feeling that God chose against you. Anger or confusion about why someone was taken so soon. Trying to make sense of a death that seems senseless.

What Scripture teaches

This saying is often meant kindly, but Scripture does not teach it. God does not need anything; He gives life and breath to everyone. Death is called “the last enemy,” not a sign of God’s greed. Jesus wept at His friend’s grave. God holds our times in His hands, and we may not understand why a loved one died, but we can know that God did not take them because He needed them more than you. He grieves with you and will one day destroy death forever.

The God who made the world and everything in it is the Lord of heaven and earth and does not live in temples made by human hands. Nor is He served by human hands, as if He needed anything, because He Himself gives everyone life and breath and everything else (Acts 17:24–25).

Also see

The last enemy to be destroyed is death (1 Corinthians 15:26).

Jesus wept (John 11:35).

He heals the brokenhearted and binds up their wounds (Psalm 147:3).

‘He will wipe away every tear from their eyes,’ and there will be no more death or mourning or crying or pain, for the former things have passed away (Revelation 21:4).

Common self-statements

  • God needed them more than I did.
  • God chose to take them from me.
  • God must not care about my pain.
  • I wasn’t important enough to keep them.
  • God wanted them in heaven, so He took them.
  • I’m angry at God for taking them.

Common logical errors

  • Mind reading: Assuming you know why God allowed the death.
  • Projection: Picturing God as needy or selfish.
  • Personalization: Feeling that God chose against you personally.
  • Accepting a saying without checking: Treating a funeral phrase as Scripture.

Challenging questions

  • Does God need anything, according to Acts 17:25?
  • What does Scripture call death?
  • How did Jesus respond to death at Lazarus’ grave?
  • Is this saying from the Bible or from well-meaning people?
  • Can I bring my anger and confusion honestly to God?
  • Do I need to know why, or can I trust God without knowing?
  • What does God promise to do with death in the end?

Truth summary

God did not take my loved one because He needed them more than I did. God needs nothing. Death is an enemy that Jesus will destroy. I may not understand why my loved one died, but I know God grieves with me, heals the brokenhearted, and will one day wipe away every tear.
