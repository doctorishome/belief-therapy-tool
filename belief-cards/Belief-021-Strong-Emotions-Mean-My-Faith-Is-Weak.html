Spiritual/Religious False Belief #21 · The Soul: Emotions, Body & Mind

“Strong emotions mean my faith is weak.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

Being told as a child to stop crying or calm down. A church culture that valued always looking happy and peaceful. Hearing that a “real Christian” should never feel afraid, sad, or upset. A family where feelings were ignored or punished. Confusing strong feelings with a lack of trust in God.

What Scripture teaches

God made us with emotions, and Scripture is full of people of strong faith who felt deeply. The psalmists poured out sorrow, fear, and confusion to God. Jesus was deeply troubled and distressed in Gethsemane. Paul admitted to fears within. Faith does not mean having no strong feelings; it means bringing those feelings to God and trusting Him with them.

Trust in Him at all times, O people; pour out your hearts before Him. God is our refuge (Psalm 62:8).

Also see

Why are you downcast, O my soul? Why the unease within me? Put your hope in God, for I will yet praise Him for the salvation of His presence (Psalm 42:5).

He took with Him Peter, James, and John, and began to be deeply troubled and distressed. Then He said to them, ‘My soul is consumed with sorrow to the point of death. Stay here and keep watch.’ (Mark 14:33–34).

For when we arrived in Macedonia, our bodies had no rest, but we were pressed from every direction—conflicts on the outside, fears within. But God, who comforts the downcast, comforted us by the arrival of Titus (2 Corinthians 7:5–6).

When I am afraid, I put my trust in You (Psalm 56:3).

Common self-statements

  • If I trusted God, I wouldn’t feel this way.
  • Christians shouldn’t cry this much.
  • My feelings prove I’m spiritually weak.
  • I have to hide how I really feel.
  • God is disappointed when I’m upset.
  • Strong Christians are always calm.

Common logical errors

  • Emotional reasoning in reverse: Concluding that strong feelings must mean weak faith.
  • Should statements: “I should never feel afraid, sad, or angry.”
  • Labeling: Calling yourself “weak” because you feel deeply.
  • Discounting the positive: Overlooking that bringing feelings to God is an act of faith.
  • Comparison: Comparing your inner life to other people’s calm appearance.

Challenging questions

  • Did Jesus feel strong emotions? Was His faith weak?
  • What did the psalmists do with their sorrow and fear?
  • Does Psalm 62:8 tell me to hide my heart or pour it out?
  • Who taught me that feelings were bad or dangerous?
  • Can I feel afraid and still trust God at the same time?
  • What would change if I saw my emotions as something to bring to God?
  • Is faith about what I feel, or about whom I trust?

Truth summary

Strong emotions do not mean my faith is weak. God made me with feelings, and Jesus Himself was deeply troubled. Faith is not the absence of strong feelings; it is bringing them honestly to God. I can pour out my heart to Him and trust Him at the same time.

My action step this week

Do one thing that tests the lie in real life.
