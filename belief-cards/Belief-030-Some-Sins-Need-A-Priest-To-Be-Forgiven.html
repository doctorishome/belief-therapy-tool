Spiritual/Religious False Belief #30 · Sin

“Some sins can’t be forgiven until I confess them to a priest.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

Church teaching that tied forgiveness of serious sins to confession with a priest. Fear of dying before you could get to confession. Shame that keeps you from going to confession, leaving you feeling stuck and unforgiven. Not knowing how to confess directly to God. Some traditions value confession to a priest as a helpful practice; the lie is that God cannot forgive you when you come to Him directly in Christ.

What Scripture teaches

Scripture shows people confessing directly to God and being forgiven. David said, “I will confess my transgressions to the LORD,” and God forgave the guilt of his sin. The tax collector simply prayed, “God, have mercy on me, a sinner,” and Jesus said he went home justified. Through Christ, you can approach God’s throne of grace yourself. Confessing to other believers is also encouraged in Scripture and can bring healing, but it is Christ who forgives.

Then I acknowledged my sin to You and did not hide my iniquity. I said, ‘I will confess my transgressions to the LORD,’ and You forgave the guilt of my sin (Psalm 32:5).

Also see

If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness (1 John 1:9).

But the tax collector stood at a distance, unwilling even to lift up his eyes to heaven. Instead, he beat his breast and said, ‘God, have mercy on me, a sinner!’ I tell you, this man, rather than the Pharisee, went home justified. For everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, but the one who humbles himself will be exalted (Luke 18:13–14).

Let us then approach the throne of grace with confidence, so that we may receive mercy and find grace to help us in our time of need (Hebrews 4:16).

Therefore confess your sins to each other and pray for each other so that you may be healed. The prayer of a righteous man has great power to prevail (James 5:16).

Common self-statements

  • God won’t forgive me until I go to confession.
  • This sin is too serious for just praying.
  • If I die before confession, I’m lost.
  • I’m too ashamed to confess, so I’m stuck.
  • My prayers alone don’t count.
  • Someone else has to declare me forgiven.

Common logical errors

  • Overgeneralization: Turning a church practice into a limit on God’s power to forgive.
  • Catastrophizing: Imagining the worst if you cannot reach a priest in time.
  • Discounting Scripture: Overlooking examples of people confessing directly to God.
  • Emotional reasoning: Concluding “I don’t feel forgiven, so I’m not.”

Challenging questions

  • What happened when David confessed directly to the LORD?
  • Did the tax collector need a priest to go home justified?
  • What does 1 John 1:9 promise when I confess?
  • Who is my great high priest?
  • Can I value confession with others and still know that God forgives me directly?
  • Is fear keeping me from God, rather than bringing me to Him?
  • What would change if I confessed to God right now?

Truth summary

I can confess my sins directly to God, and He is faithful and just to forgive me. Jesus is my great high priest, and through Him I can come to God’s throne of grace. Confessing to other believers can bring healing, and I may choose to do that, but God’s forgiveness is not out of my reach. When I confess to Him, He forgives.
