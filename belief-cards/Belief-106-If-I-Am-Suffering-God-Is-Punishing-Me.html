Spiritual/Religious False Belief #106 · Suffering

“If I am suffering, God is punishing me.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

Teaching that every hardship is God’s punishment for sin. Friends who asked, “What did you do to deserve this?” A guilty conscience that looks for reasons. Childhood punishment that taught you pain always means you did wrong. Wanting an explanation for suffering.

What Scripture teaches

Sometimes suffering is a natural consequence of sin, and God does lovingly discipline His children for their good. But Scripture does not teach that all suffering is punishment. Job was blameless, yet he suffered greatly, and God rebuked the friends who said it was his fault. Jesus said the man born blind did not suffer because of anyone’s sin. For believers, the punishment for sin fell on Jesus. Suffering is part of living in a broken world, and God is with you in it.

And His disciples asked Him, ‘Rabbi, who sinned, this man or his parents, that he was born blind?’ Jesus answered, ‘Neither this man nor his parents sinned, but this happened so that the works of God would be displayed in him.’ (John 9:2–3).

After the LORD had spoken these words to Job, He said to Eliphaz the Temanite, ‘My wrath is kindled against you and your two friends. For you have not spoken about Me accurately, as My servant Job has.’ (Job 42:7).

But He was pierced for our transgressions, He was crushed for our iniquities; the punishment that brought us peace was upon Him, and by His stripes we are healed (Isaiah 53:5).

Our fathers disciplined us for a short time as they thought best, but God disciplines us for our good, so that we may share in His holiness. No discipline seems enjoyable at the time, but painful. Later on, however, it yields a harvest of righteousness and peace to those who have been trained by it (Hebrews 12:10–11).

For the Lord will not cast us off forever. Even if He causes grief, He will show compassion according to His abundant loving devotion. For He does not willingly afflict or grieve the sons of men (Lamentations 3:31–33).

Common self-statements

  • God is punishing me for something.
  • I must have done something to deserve this.
  • This pain proves God is angry with me.
  • If I were living right, this wouldn’t happen.
  • I have to figure out what sin caused this.
  • God wants me to suffer.

Common logical errors

  • Personalization: Assuming suffering is always about something you did.
  • Jumping to conclusions: Deciding the cause without evidence.
  • Overgeneralization: Turning “sin can bring consequences” into “all suffering is punishment.”
  • Projection: Picturing God as eager to punish.

Challenging questions

  • What did Jesus say about the man born blind?
  • Was Job suffering because of his sin?
  • What did God say to Job’s friends who blamed him?
  • Where did the punishment for my sin go, according to Isaiah 53:5?
  • Is there a clear sin I need to confess, or am I searching for a reason?
  • What does Lamentations 3:33 say about God’s heart?
  • Can I trust God’s love even in my suffering?

Truth summary

My suffering does not automatically mean God is punishing me. Job suffered though he was blameless, and Jesus said the blind man’s condition was not caused by sin. For those in Christ, the punishment for sin fell on Jesus. God may discipline me for my good, but He does not willingly afflict me. He is with me in my suffering.
