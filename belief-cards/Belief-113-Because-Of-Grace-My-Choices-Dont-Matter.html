Spiritual/Religious False Belief #113 · Grace & Works

“Because of grace, my choices don’t matter.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Where it often comes from

Reacting against legalism by swinging to the other extreme. Hearing “God will forgive anyway” used to excuse sin. Wanting freedom without responsibility. Teaching that focused on grace but never on growth. Habits that are hard to change, so it feels easier to say they don’t matter.

What Scripture teaches

Grace is God’s free gift, and it never depends on our performance. But grace does not make our choices meaningless. Paul asked, “Shall we continue in sin so that grace may increase? Certainly not!” The grace of God teaches us to say no to ungodliness and to live upright lives. We are called to freedom, but not to use it for the flesh; rather, to serve one another in love. Grace saves us and also changes us.

What then shall we say? Shall we continue in sin so that grace may increase? Certainly not! How can we who died to sin live in it any longer? (Romans 6:1–2).

Also see

For the grace of God has appeared, bringing salvation to everyone. It instructs us to renounce ungodliness and worldly passions, and to live sensible, upright, and godly lives in the present age (Titus 2:11–12).

For you, brothers, were called to freedom; but do not use your freedom as an opportunity for the flesh. Rather, serve one another in love (Galatians 5:13).

For sin shall not be your master, because you are not under law, but under grace (Romans 6:14).

For we are God’s workmanship, created in Christ Jesus to do good works, which God prepared in advance as our way of life (Ephesians 2:10).

Common self-statements

  • God will forgive me anyway, so it doesn’t matter.
  • Grace means I don’t have to change.
  • My choices don’t affect my relationship with God.
  • Obedience is legalism.
  • I can live however I want.
  • Sin doesn’t really hurt anyone.

Common logical errors

  • All-or-nothing thinking: Believing you must choose between grace and obedience.
  • Minimizing: Downplaying the real harm sin causes.
  • Rationalizing: Using grace as an excuse for choices you know are wrong.
  • Mental filter: Overlooking Scripture’s teaching that grace trains us to live well.

Challenging questions

  • How did Paul answer, “Shall we continue in sin so that grace may increase?”
  • What does the grace of God teach us, according to Titus 2:12?
  • What is freedom in Christ for, according to Galatians 5:13?
  • Do my choices affect me and the people I love?
  • Is grace something to presume on or something to respond to?
  • What does Ephesians 2:10 say I was created for?
  • What choice do I need to take more seriously?

Truth summary

Grace is free, but my choices still matter. God’s grace saves me and also teaches me to say no to ungodliness and live a godly life. I am free in Christ, not to indulge the flesh but to serve others in love. My choices affect me, others, and my closeness with God, and grace gives me power to choose well.
