When to Use This Form

Screen every client at intake (ages 12 and up) and again whenever there is a crisis, a major loss, worsening depression, new warning signs, or a care transition. Ask directly and calmly; asking about suicide does not plant the idea. If the client is in immediate danger, stop the form, stay with the client, and call 911 or 988.

Part 4  Risk Level and Actions Taken

Guide based on the Columbia screener triage and SAMHSA SAFE-T. Final risk level is the counselor’s clinical judgment.

Risk levelTypical findingsUsual response
Low“Yes” to question 1 or 2 only; no method, intent, plan, or recent behavior; protective factors present.Safety plan; crisis numbers; outpatient follow-up; re-screen next visit.
Moderate“Yes” to question 3, or suicidal behavior more than 3 months ago; several risk factors.Safety plan; lethal means counseling; consultation; more frequent sessions; consider psychiatric evaluation.
High“Yes” to question 4 or 5, or suicidal behavior in the past 3 months.Do not leave client alone; immediate evaluation for higher level of care (crisis team, emergency department, 911/988); lethal means removed.

Part 5  Lethal Means Counseling Notes

Ask about access to firearms, medications (including stockpiles), and other methods named by the client. Discuss safe storage or temporary removal, ideally with a family member’s help. Reducing access during a crisis saves lives.
